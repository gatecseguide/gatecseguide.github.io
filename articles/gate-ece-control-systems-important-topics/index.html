Control Systems for GATE ECE 2027: Important Topics

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 14 Nov 20268 min read

Control Systems averaged 8.0 marks across the five GATE EC papers counted question by question for this site (2010, 2019, 2020, 2025 and 2026). It is one of the smaller sections by marks, and one of the steadiest: the range was 6 to 10 marks, and no counted paper had fewer than four questions or more than six.

It is also the section where each question costs the most. Sixteen of its 24 counted questions were worth 2 marks, an average of 1.67 marks a question, the heaviest in the paper. A thin pass does not work here, because four or five questions carry the whole section.

Key takeaways

  • 24 questions and 40 marks across five papers, 8 of them 1-mark and 16 of them 2-mark.
  • Routh-Hurwitz and the state variable model each appear in four of five papers, both in 2025 and 2026. Together they are 15 of the 40 marks.
  • Every numerical-answer question in the section asked for a gain K. Four papers, four numerical questions, four values of K.
  • PID controller is new to the 2027 syllabus and has no three-term counted precedent, but its individual terms have been tested three times since 2019.
  • The 2019 paper, set by IIT Madras which also sets GATE 2027, asked no root-locus and no Nyquist question, and the only second-order transient question in the counted set.
  • Question numbers follow the book's count, which runs 1 to 65 through each paper. The 2019 and 2020 papers restart their technical questions at 1, so subtract 10 to find them there.

Ten bands cover all 24 questions with nothing left over, and no band went completely untested. Nothing here behaves like a retired topic: state-space, Routh, block diagrams and compensators were all in the 2026 paper.

Routh is the numerical question. Three of the four numerical answers ask for the KKK that puts the system at marginal stability or puts poles on the imaginary axis (2019 Q.52, 2020 Q.33, 2026 Q.59). The fourth asks for the KKK that gives a stated steady-state error to a ramp (2020 Q.65). The harder Routh item is the one that ends in a row of zeros and needs the auxiliary polynomial, as in 2025 Q.37.

State-space comes in pairs of directions. Choose AAA and CCC to realise a given transfer function (2019 Q.43), or go the other way through C(sI−A)−1BC(sI - A)^{-1}BC(sI−A)−1B (2026 Q.41). 2025 Q.53 asked whether the states stay bounded for bounded inputs, which is where a stable transfer function can hide an unstable mode.

Two bands are overdue. Bode reading and second-order transient response were last counted in 2019, three papers ago, and 2019 is the IIT Madras paper. Both could also appear under a Networks, Signals and Systems label, so prepare the skill without assuming which column the marks will land in.

What the 2027 syllabus changed

The 2026 line read "Lag, lead and lag-lead compensation". The 2027 line reads "compensators, PID controller". Two things happened, and they pull in different directions. The full comparison is in the GATE ECE 2027 syllabus changes guide.

PID was added. It is the only genuinely new named item in Control Systems. No counted paper contains a three-term PID controller. But the terms have been tested separately. Integral action appears in 2019 Q.52, an integral compensator Ks\displaystyle \frac{K}{s}sK​ inside a Routh question, and in 2025 Q.36, which compares an integral controller with a PD controller. The derivative gain lands on the s2s^2s2 coefficient, the proportional gain on sss and the integral gain on the constant. That is the same coefficient-matching and Routh work every counted controller question already used. Nothing in the counted evidence points to Ziegler-Nichols tables.

The named lag, lead and lag-lead trio was de-specified. The word "compensators" survives, so a lead network and its maximum phase lead are still fair game. It is simply no longer a third of a named list. None of the five counted papers asked a classical lag or lead design to a phase-margin specification.

Phase margin itself has also been added to the Analog Circuits syllabus for op-amp compensation, so the same frequency-response skill now serves two sections. Syllabus text can be revised; confirm the current PDF on the official GATE 2027 site.

The 2019 paper and what it suggests

2019 gave Control Systems 9 marks from only five questions, four of them 2-mark, the highest marks per question of the five papers. It asked the only second-order transient question in the counted set (Q.41, the time for a step response to reach 94 per cent of its final value). It asked no root-locus and no Nyquist question. And three of its five questions (Q.16, Q.41, Q.42) could equally be counted under Networks, Signals and Systems.

One paper of five questions is a signal, not a law. It is far too small to conclude that IIT Madras avoids graphical methods, and both root locus and Nyquist appeared in 2020 and 2025. The usable lesson is narrower. Its controller question, Q.52, was a Routh question wearing a compensator's clothes, which is the natural shape for a PID question that has one clean answer.

How to prepare it

  1. Routh-Hurwitz, including the range of KKK for stability, the marginal KKK, and the row-of-zeros case.
  2. State-space: canonical realisations, C(sI−A)−1B+DC(sI - A)^{-1}B + DC(sI−A)−1B+D, and the difference between a bounded output and bounded states.
  3. Block diagram reduction and Mason's formula. Mechanical, and cheap marks once drilled.
  4. A controller in the forward path: coefficient matching for pole placement, then the PID characteristic equation above.
  5. Steady-state error: system type and the error constants.
  6. Root locus, Nyquist and Bode to the level of breakaway points, the gain at a stated pole, encirclements and crossover frequencies.
  7. Second-order transient response, which the 2027 institute asked last time it set the paper.

The traps are conventions rather than algebra: the sign in Z=P−NZ = P - NZ=P−N on a Nyquist plot, a gain margin reported as a ratio where decibels were asked, a breakaway root that does not lie on the locus, and the belief that a stable transfer function means bounded states. A wrong multiple-choice answer costs 13\displaystyle \frac{1}{3}31​ mark on a 1-mark question and 23\displaystyle \frac{2}{3}32​ mark on a 2-mark one, which in a section this heavy adds up quickly.

Frequently asked questions

How many marks is Control Systems in GATE ECE?

Across the five EC papers counted for this site it carried 40 marks in 24 questions, a mean of 8.0 marks a paper. By year it was 8, 9, 6, 10 and 7 marks for 2010, 2019, 2020, 2025 and 2026. Two-thirds of its questions were worth 2 marks, so it averages 1.67 marks a question, the heaviest in the paper.

Is the PID controller new in the GATE ECE 2027 syllabus?

Yes. PID controller is the one genuinely new item in the Control Systems text for 2027, and no counted paper contains a three-term PID question. Its separate terms have been tested, though. Integral action appears in 2019 Q.52 and 2025 Q.36, and derivative action in 2025 Q.36 and 2026 Q.40. Prepare it through the characteristic equation.

Are lag and lead compensators removed from GATE ECE 2027?

Not removed, de-specified. The 2026 line named lag, lead and lag-lead compensation; the 2027 line says compensators and adds the PID controller. A lead network is still a compensator and still examinable. None of the five counted papers asked a classical lag or lead design to a phase-margin specification, so it was never the heavy topic its old wording suggested.

Which control systems topics are most important for GATE ECE?

Routh-Hurwitz and the state variable model each appear in four of the five counted papers, and both were in 2025 and 2026. Together they account for 15 of the section's 40 marks. Block diagram reduction appears in three papers, and a controller or compensator in the forward path appears in four once questions are read for substance.

What kind of numerical questions come from control systems in GATE ECE?

On the counted evidence, a gain. All four numerical-answer questions in the section, 2019 Q.52, 2020 Q.33, 2020 Q.65 and 2026 Q.59, ask for a value of K, three of them through the characteristic equation and a Routh array. There is no counted numerical question asking for a settling time, a margin in decibels or a frequency.
