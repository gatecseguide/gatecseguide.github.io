M.Tech Admission After GATE CSE: COAP, CCMT and Direct Portals

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 21 Sep 20268 min read

The GATE result is the start of a second process, not the end of the first. For M.Tech admission there are three separate routes — COAP for IISc and the IITs, CCMT for the NITs and several other centrally funded institutes, and direct portals for everyone outside both. They run on different calendars with different rules, and missing a registration window closes that route for the year. This guide explains how each one works.

Key takeaways

  • COAP is an offer acceptance portal, not an application portal. You apply to each IIT and to IISc separately.
  • CCMT is a full centralised counselling: one registration, one locked choice list, a common seat allotment.
  • The two systems run in parallel with separate fees and separate deadlines. Aiming at both means registering for both.
  • The brochure states that Ministry of Education guidelines prescribe a minimum of 70% weightage to GATE performance in admission.
  • This guide gives the process and the rounds. It does not give institute cutoffs, because those are published by each counselling body after each round.

Route 1: COAP, for IISc and the IITs

COAP stands for Common Offer Acceptance Portal. The problem it solves is simple: before it existed, a candidate holding offers from several IITs blocked seats at all of them while deciding. COAP puts every participating institute's offer into one window, so the candidate responds once per round and released seats move down the queue immediately.

Two facts about COAP trip people up every year.

It does not take your application. You apply to each institute's own M.Tech admission portal, within that institute's own deadline, and you meet that institute's own eligibility requirements. COAP only carries the offers that result.

Its address changes. A different IIT coordinates COAP each cycle, and the portal moves with it — COAP 2026, for instance, ran at coap2026.iitr.ac.in under IIT Roorkee. Search for the current year's portal rather than bookmarking last year's.

COAP runs in a sequence of rounds. In each round you see the offers you currently hold and choose how to respond. The options that have been used are:

Option What it does
Accept and Freeze You take this offer. You leave the process; the institute that made the offer takes over
Retain and Wait You keep this offer while staying in contention for something you prefer in a later round
Reject and Wait You decline this offer and stay in the process for later rounds

In the later rounds the portal narrows these choices — at a point set by each year's schedule, the retain option is withdrawn and candidates must accept or reject. The exact round at which that happens is stated in the current COAP information brochure, so read it when the portal opens.

Once you choose Accept and Freeze, the process moves to the institute. Official IIT Bombay instructions for its 2026 M.Tech offers, for example, direct candidates to COAP and then require an online fee payment within a stated window, with the offer forfeited if the payment is not made. Every institute publishes its own equivalent instruction; read the one that made you the offer.

Route 2: CCMT, for NITs, IIEST and several IIITs and CFTIs

CCMT — Centralized Counselling for M.Tech./M.Arch./M.Plan. Admissions — has run since 2012 and works the way JoSAA does for undergraduates. One registration, one choice list, a common allotment. It is hosted by the National Informatics Centre at ccmt.admissions.nic.in, and a different NIT coordinates each cycle; CCMT 2026 was coordinated by NIT Agartala.

In CCMT 2026, the structure was:

  • Eligibility: a valid GATE score from 2024, 2025 or 2026, plus a minimum of 6.5 CGPA or 60% in the qualifying degree for GEN, GEN-EWS and OBC candidates, and 6.0 CGPA or 55% for SC, ST and PwD candidates.
  • Scope: full-time seats carrying the GATE scholarship. Sponsored seats and part-time programmes were outside CCMT and had to be pursued with the institute directly.
  • Rounds: three regular rounds, then two special rounds for seats left over, then one National Spot Round.
  • Merit: prepared on the GATE score of the year the candidate selects at registration. Ties were broken first by All India Rank divided by the total number of candidates who appeared in that GATE paper, then by date of birth, then by a random number.

Choice filling is the part to take seriously. Choices can be added, removed and reordered until the deadline, after which they lock — automatically, using your last saved list, if you do not lock them yourself. Once locked, they cannot be changed under any circumstance, and a candidate with an empty list is not considered for allotment at all.

After each allotment you declare a willingness:

Willingness Effect in the next round
Float Considered for all better-preferred choices, across every institute
Slide Considered for better choices within the allotted institute only
Freeze Keep this seat; no upgrade sought
Surrender and participate Give up the seat and re-enter the next regular round, with no claim on the surrendered seat

Willingness can be changed only in the downgrade direction — Float to Slide, Float or Slide to Freeze — never back towards Float.

CCMT also charges a Seat Acceptance Fee once a seat is allotted and, after the regular rounds, a Partial Admission Fee to confirm it; both are adjusted against the institute's admission fee, and failing to pay either cancels the allotment. The amounts, dates and round structure are set afresh every cycle, so treat the description above as CCMT 2026 and confirm the current year on the official portal.

Route 3: direct portals

Plenty of good CS departments sit outside both systems — several IIITs, state and private universities, and specialised institutes. They advertise their own M.Tech admissions, take applications on their own portals and publish their own schedules. Sponsored and part-time seats at CCMT institutes also fall here.

There is no central listing of these, so the only reliable method is to decide which departments you want and check their admissions pages from March onward.

What it is worth, and what it costs you

A GATE score used for admission also determines financial assistance. The official opportunities page states the postgraduate scholarship as ₹12,400 per month for M.Tech, typically for 22 months, and ₹37,000 per month for the first two years of a direct doctoral programme, rising to ₹42,000 for years three to five. The brochure adds that Ministry of Education guidelines prescribe a minimum of 70% weightage to GATE performance in admission, with the remainder for the institute's test or interview and the academic record.

That last line is the one to internalise while preparing. Seventy percent of the decision is a number you generate in three hours in February. How the GATE score is calculated and normalised explains how your marks become that number, and GATE CSE qualifying marks and cutoffs explained explains why clearing the qualifying threshold is nowhere near enough for these queues.

Your calendar after the result

GATE 2027 results are scheduled for 19 March 2027; the surrounding dates are in GATE 2027 exam dates. From there:

  1. Download and save the scorecard as soon as the free window opens.
  2. Track the M.Tech admission pages of the IITs and IISc departments you want, and apply to each within its own deadline.
  3. Register on COAP when the current year's portal opens, and respond in every round — no response is treated as no response.
  4. Register on CCMT separately, build a long and honest choice list, and lock it deliberately.
  5. Keep documents ready: degree marksheets, category certificate with a valid issue date, and the GATE scorecard.
  6. Decide in advance whether you are also pursuing PSU recruitment through GATE, since the two run on overlapping timelines.

Frequently asked questions

What is COAP in M.Tech admission?

COAP is the Common Offer Acceptance Portal, a single place where IISc Bengaluru and the IITs publish M.Tech offers so a candidate sees all of them in one window and responds round by round. It is not an application portal. You still apply separately to each institute you want an offer from.

What is the difference between COAP and CCMT?

COAP handles offers from IISc and the IITs, where you apply to each institute yourself. CCMT is a centralised counselling that covers NITs, IIEST Shibpur and selected IIITs and other centrally funded institutes, where one registration and one locked choice list feed a common seat allotment.

Do I need to register separately for COAP and CCMT?

Yes. They are run by different coordinating institutes with different schedules, fees and rules. A candidate aiming at both IITs and NITs registers on both. Missing either registration window closes that route for the year, regardless of how good the GATE score is.

Which GATE years does CCMT accept?

CCMT 2026 accepted valid GATE scores from 2024, 2025 and 2026, which matches the three-year validity of a GATE scorecard. The accepted years are restated in each cycle's information brochure, so a GATE 2027 candidate should confirm them on the official CCMT site rather than assuming.

What do Float, Slide and Freeze mean in CCMT?

Float keeps you in the running for all better-preferred choices across institutes. Slide considers you only for better choices within the same allotted institute. Freeze means you accept the current seat and stop seeking upgrades. Changes are allowed in the downgrade direction only, never back towards Float.

How much does GATE count in M.Tech admission?

The official brochure says the Ministry of Education guidelines prescribe a minimum of 70 percent weightage to GATE performance, with the rest for a test or interview and academic record. Institutes may also set a minimum passing mark in that test or interview.
