GATE DA Previous Year Papers Analysis (2024-2026)

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 9 Nov 20266 min read

There are only three GATE DA papers, 2024, 2025 and 2026, because the paper was first held in 2024. Counted question by question against the official keys, they show a fixed frame and a technical split that moves a lot. This guide explains how the three were counted, what the combined record shows, and the limits of what three papers can tell you.

Key takeaways

  • The whole DA record is three papers, one a year: 195 questions and 300 marks.
  • Each was set by a different institute: IISc Bengaluru, IIT Roorkee and IIT Guwahati. IIT Madras sets 2027 and has never set a DA paper.
  • All three papers close on the same frame: 65 questions, 100 marks, 30 one-mark and 35 two-mark questions, General Aptitude at 15.
  • No counted mark falls outside the 2027 syllabus.
  • Twenty-three of 165 technical questions could sit in two sections; the count records both.
  • Three papers show patterns, not trends.

What "counted" means

A paper is counted when every question is read from the organising institute's own question paper, and its marks, type and answer are taken from that year's official answer key. The GATE DA 2027 book did this for all three papers and placed each question on a section and topic of the 2027 syllabus.

A straddle is a question that could honestly belong to two sections, such as a principal component question that is also an eigenvalue question. The book applied fixed boundary rules, placed the question, and recorded the other section beside it.

Each year has one DA paper, so a year and a question number identify one question exactly. A citation such as 2025 (Q35) means the paper's own printed number.

Remember: Anything sold as a "DA previous year question" from before 2024 comes from another paper.

Every count had to close

A count is only trustworthy if its totals match the paper. Each of the three had to pass these checks, with nothing moved or merged to force a total.

Worked example: the closing arithmetic

The layout puts Q1 to Q5 and Q11 to Q35 at one mark, and Q6 to Q10 and Q36 to Q65 at two. So:

one-mark questions=5+25=30two-mark questions=5+30=35total marks=30×1+35×2=100\begin{aligned} \text{one-mark questions} &= 5 + 25 = 30 \\ \text{two-mark questions} &= 5 + 30 = 35 \\ \text{total marks} &= 30 \times 1 + 35 \times 2 = 100 \end{aligned}one-mark questionstwo-mark questionstotal marks​=5+25=30=5+30=35=30×1+35×2=100​

General Aptitude is 5×1+5×2=155 \times 1 + 5 \times 2 = 155×1+5×2=15, which leaves 100−15=85100 - 15 = 85100−15=85 technical marks. All three papers closed on exactly these numbers, and every row matched the official key.

Four entries in the keys worth knowing

  • 2024 (Q59) is marked MTA, marks to all. The joint density it printed integrates to 4, not 1. It is counted as the two marks of Probability and Statistics it was set as.
  • 2026 (Q59), a tuple count, is keyed as the range 2 to 3.
  • 2025 (Q27), 2026 (Q23) and 2026 (Q51) are multiple-select questions with exactly one correct option.

When you practise from these papers, check your answers against the official keys, not against a solved-paper book.

Trap: Do not lose time on 2024 (Q59) as if it had a clean answer. The key gave everyone the marks because the question was flawed.

What the combined record shows

A fixed frame

The size, the mark blocks, the General Aptitude block and the presence of all three question types held under three organisers without exception. All thirty General Aptitude questions were MCQs, so every MSQ and NAT sat in the technical 85 marks.

A moving technical split

The section totals moved a lot. Summing the section-by-section differences, consecutive papers differed by 26 technical marks out of 85, and 2024 and 2026 by 34. Database went 7, 11, 18 and Calculus went 8, 11, 2.

A core that keeps returning

Twenty-one technical topic groups drew marks in all three papers, carrying 155 of the 255 technical marks. Examples are Python code traces, continuous random variables, logic and search, and relational algebra.

No material outside the syllabus

Every counted technical question sits on a phrase the 2027 syllabus names. Two sit at the edge: 2024 (Q51), on topological orderings, and 2026 (Q35), a double geometric series. Both are counted at their lean, in Programming and Calculus.

What three papers cannot show

They cannot show a trend

Probability and Statistics went 15, 19, 21 and Database went 7, 11, 18. That looks like growth. Now count how often chance alone produces it.

Three different numbers can be arranged in 3!=63! = 63!=6 orders. Exactly two of them, rising and falling, move the same way in both steps. So a random shuffle looks like a "trend" with probability:

26=13\frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}62​=31​

Across seven sections, about 7×13≈2.3\displaystyle 7 \times \tfrac{1}{3} \approx 2.37×31​≈2.3 would look like trends by chance. The record shows two. Three papers cannot tell those apart from noise.

They cannot show an institute's style

Each institute set one paper in one year, so the organiser and the year are the same variable. Whether 2024's 20 marks of Programming reflect IISc Bengaluru, a first-year paper or chance cannot be separated. IIT Madras has never set DA, as the guide to the 2027 organising institute explains.

In one line: Use the three papers to learn how a topic is asked, not to forecast whether it will be.

How to use the three papers

  1. Sit 2024 early, timed, as a map of question shapes.
  2. Hold 2025 and 2026 back as full timed papers once four or five sections are learned.
  3. Mark them with the official rules, including negative marking on MCQs. The marking scheme guide, common to every GATE paper, sets these out.
  4. Analyse by section, not by total.

General Aptitude was the same 15 marks in all three papers, and the General Aptitude guide covers its preparation. The score calculation guide shows how raw marks become a GATE score.

This guide gives the method and the conclusions. The GATE DA 2027 book prints every question of the three papers by section, topic and type, with every boundary decision and what moving it would change.

Quick revision

  1. Three DA papers only: 2024, 2025, 2026; 195 questions, 300 marks.
  2. Setters: IISc Bengaluru, IIT Roorkee, IIT Guwahati; IIT Madras sets 2027.
  3. Fixed frame: 65 questions, 100 marks, 30 one-mark, 35 two-mark, GA 15.
  4. Question types: 37/12/16, 35/18/12, 33/14/18 (MCQ/MSQ/NAT).
  5. 2024 (Q59) was marks to all; three MSQs had one correct option.
  6. No counted mark outside the 2027 syllabus.
  7. Patterns, not trends: a rise-rise or fall-fall comes by chance one time in three.

Frequently asked questions

How many GATE DA previous year papers are there?

Three. GATE first set the DA paper in 2024, and there has been one paper a year since, in 2024, 2025 and 2026. That is 195 questions and 300 marks in all. Any DA question dated before 2024 comes from some other paper, not from DA.

Where can I download GATE DA previous year papers?

From the organising institutes' own portals. The 2026 paper and key are on the GATE 2026 portal of IIT Guwahati, the 2025 files on the GATE 2025 portal of IIT Roorkee, and the 2024 paper set by IISc Bengaluru is in the archive the GATE 2026 portal hosts. Use the official keys for answers.

Which institutes set the GATE DA papers?

IISc Bengaluru set the 2024 paper, IIT Roorkee the 2025 paper and IIT Guwahati the 2026 paper. GATE 2027 is organised by IIT Madras, which has never set a DA paper, so there is no institute-matched evidence for 2027 at all.

Are all GATE DA previous year questions still in the syllabus?

Yes. Every one of the 165 counted technical questions was placed on a phrase the 2027 syllabus names, so no mark falls outside it. Two questions sit at the edge, a topological ordering in 2024 and a double geometric series in 2026, but both rest on named skills.

Do GATE DA papers show any trends?

No trend can be read from three papers. Two sections rose in both steps, but three random numbers rise or fall twice in a row one time in three, so two or three of seven sections would look like trends by chance. The papers show patterns of what recurs, not directions.
