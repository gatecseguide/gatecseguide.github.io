GATE DA Probability and Statistics Formula Sheet with Traps

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 4 Oct 20267 min read

The probability and statistics formulas you need for GATE DA fit on a few tables: counting and probability rules, expectation and variance, a dozen distributions, and the sampling results behind confidence intervals and tests. This sheet gives each formula with its condition, a one-line example of my own and the trap that costs marks. Every formula is checked against the last-minute sheet in the GATE DA 2027 book.

Key takeaways

  • Write the conditional the question asks for, P(A∣B)P(A \mid B)P(A∣B) or P(B∣A)P(B \mid A)P(B∣A), before you substitute anything.
  • Variance scales by the square: Var⁡(aX+b)=a2Var⁡(X)\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}(X)Var(aX+b)=a2Var(X).
  • The sample variance divides by n−1n - 1n−1; the population form divides by nnn.
  • Learn each distribution as a set of three: its PMF or PDF, its mean and its variance.
  • Interval and test questions turn on one choice: zzz when σ\sigmaσ is known, ttt with n−1n - 1n−1 degrees of freedom when it is not.
  • No counted paper (2024 to 2026) has examined confidence intervals or the zzz, ttt and chi-squared tests, but the syllabus names them, so prepare them.

The terms this sheet uses

A random variable is a number attached to each outcome of an experiment. A discrete one has a probability mass function (PMF), P(X=k)P(X = k)P(X=k). A continuous one has a probability density function (PDF), f(x)f(x)f(x), whose area over an interval is a probability.

The cumulative distribution function (CDF) is F(x)=P(X≤x)F(x) = P(X \le x)F(x)=P(X≤x). It never decreases and runs from 0 to 1. A sample is the data you observe; the population is what it was drawn from. xˉ\bar{x}xˉ is the sample mean, sss the sample standard deviation, and μ\muμ and σ\sigmaσ their population counterparts. ln⁡\lnln is the natural logarithm.

Sampling and the central limit theorem

The central limit theorem says a sum or mean of many independent draws is close to normal, whatever the original shape, provided the variance is finite.

A Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis, with probability α\alphaα. A Type II error keeps a false one, with probability β\betaβ; the power is 1−β1 - \beta1−β. The p-value is the probability, under the null, of a statistic at least as extreme as the one observed. Reject when p<αp < \alphap<α.

In one line: Pick zzz or ttt by whether σ\sigmaσ is known, count the degrees of freedom, then compare with the critical value.

  2. Var⁡(aX+b)=a2Var⁡(X)\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}(X)Var(aX+b)=a2Var(X), and s2s^2s2 divides by n−1n - 1n−1.

Frequently asked questions

What is the variance of an exponential distribution?

For Exponential(λ)\text{Exponential}(\lambda)Exponential(λ) the mean is 1λ\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda}λ1​ and the variance is 1λ2\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda^2}λ21​. The tail is P(X>x)=e−λxP(X > x) = e^{-\lambda x}P(X>x)=e−λx and the median is ln⁡2λ\displaystyle \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda}λln2​. A common slip is to give 1λ\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda}λ1​ as the variance. The exponential is also memoryless, a property it shares only with the geometric distribution among the standard distributions.

What is the difference between independent and mutually exclusive events?

Independent events satisfy P(A∩B)=P(A) P(B)P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B)P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B), so knowing one tells you nothing about the other. Mutually exclusive events satisfy P(A∩B)=0P(A \cap B) = 0P(A∩B)=0, so one happening rules out the other. Two events that both have non-zero probability can never be both independent and mutually exclusive, which is a frequent multiple-select trap.

Which value of z is used for a 95 per cent confidence interval?

A two-sided 95 per cent interval uses z=1.96z = 1.96z=1.96, because Φ(1.96)=0.975\Phi(1.96) = 0.975Φ(1.96)=0.975. A one-sided 5 per cent test uses 1.645 and a 99 per cent two-sided interval uses 2.576. When the population standard deviation is unknown, replace zzz with the ttt value for n−1n - 1n−1 degrees of freedom and use the sample standard deviation sss.

How many degrees of freedom does a chi-squared test have?

A goodness-of-fit test with kkk categories has k−1k - 1k−1 degrees of freedom, minus one more for every parameter you estimate from the data. A test of independence on an r×cr \times cr×c table has (r−1)(c−1)(r - 1)(c - 1)(r−1)(c−1). A test for one variance uses n−1n - 1n−1. The expected count in a cell is the row total times the column total, divided by the grand total.

Does zero correlation mean two variables are independent?

No. Independence implies zero correlation, but zero correlation does not imply independence. Correlation measures only linear association. If XXX is uniform on (−1,1)(-1, 1)(−1,1) and Y=X2Y = X^2Y=X2, then YYY is completely determined by XXX, yet their correlation is zero. So a zero correlation only rules out a linear relationship, never every kind of dependence.
