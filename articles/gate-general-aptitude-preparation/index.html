GATE General Aptitude: How to Score the 15 Easiest Marks

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 18 Sep 20268 min read

GATE General Aptitude is 10 questions and 15 marks, shared by every GATE paper, and analysts have rated it the easiest section of the CS paper year after year. That makes it the most avoidable place to lose marks. This guide covers the 2027 GA syllabus, the question types that keep repeating, the traps behind most lost marks and a timed plan for attempting the section.

Key takeaways

  • GA has had 10 questions and 15 marks (five 1-mark, five 2-mark) in every CS paper since 2010.
  • The four-part GA syllabus (Verbal, Quantitative, Analytical, Spatial) has been unchanged since 2021, and the 2027 text is identical to 2026.
  • A typical recent section has 3–4 verbal, 3–4 quantitative (one of them data interpretation), 1–2 analytical and 1 spatial item.
  • Reading comprehension, grammar, quantitative word problems, data interpretation and one spatial item have appeared in essentially every GA section since 2021.
  • A realistic target is 13–15 marks in 15–18 minutes, which also buys time for the 85 technical marks.

The GATE 2027 General Aptitude syllabus

The official GA syllabus has four parts:

  • Verbal Aptitude: basic English grammar (tenses, articles, adjectives, prepositions, conjunctions, verb-noun agreement, other parts of speech); basic vocabulary (words, idioms, phrases in context); reading and comprehension; narrative sequencing.
  • Quantitative Aptitude: data interpretation (bar graphs, pie charts, other graphs, 2-D and 3-D plots, maps, tables); numerical computation and estimation (ratios, percentages, powers, exponents and logarithms, permutations and combinations, series); mensuration and geometry; elementary statistics and probability.
  • Analytical Aptitude: logic (deduction and induction); analogy; numerical relations and reasoning.
  • Spatial Aptitude: transformation of shapes (translation, rotation, scaling, mirroring, assembling, grouping); paper folding, cutting, patterns in 2 and 3 dimensions.

This structure arrived with the GATE 2021 restructure, which also introduced MSQs. Logical reasoning had already been appearing before 2021, but figure-based spatial items are a genuinely post-2021 addition.

Which GA question types repeat

The frequency bands below come from the book's reconstruction of recent GA sections from previous-year question repositories, so treat them as approximate.

Every year

  1. Reading comprehension with an inference question. A neutral 80–200 word passage and a "which can be inferred" or "main point" question, usually 2 marks.
  2. Grammar or sentence completion. Two blanks, four option pairs, testing agreement, tense or prepositions. Usually 1 mark.
  3. Quantitative word problems. Two or three items from percentages, ratios, work and time, speed, mixtures, ages, permutations and combinations, probability or logarithms.
  4. Data interpretation. One 2-mark item on a table, bar chart or pie chart. It is the most natural home for an MSQ.
  5. Spatial reasoning. One figure item: mirror image, rotation, paper folding or assembling pieces.

Most years

Word analogy, para-jumbles, number series, mensuration, syllogisms, and a defined-operation or pattern puzzle. Across any two consecutive papers, you are likely to meet each of these at least once.

Occasionally

Seating arrangements, direction sense, coding–decoding, blood relations, clocks and calendars, mean and median, and 3-D plots or maps. They are in the syllabus but not in every paper.

The traps behind most lost GA marks

GA questions are easy in concept, so marks are lost to a stable set of traps.

  • Successive percentages multiply. A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss, not zero change.
  • Units. Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18. In train problems, add the lengths of both trains.
  • "At least one." Use the complement: total minus "none". Counting directly tends to over-count.
  • Inference versus stated fact. An option that restates a passage sentence is stated, not inferred. An option that goes beyond the passage is neither. The correct inference is supported by the passage but not written in it.
  • Para-jumble openers. The opening sentence has no backward reference such as "this", "they", "then" or "however". Chain the rest by pronouns and time markers.
  • "Necessarily true." A conclusion that holds in one valid arrangement but fails in another is not necessarily true. Check every arrangement.
  • Mirror versus rotation. A left-right mirror reverses each row; a 180° rotation reverses the row order and each row. Sketch the grid when unsure.
  • Base year in data interpretation. Growth is measured from the earlier year. A share needs that year's total.
  • Grammar pairs. Fewer versus less, its versus it's, and "between you and me" show up in "which sentences are correct" items.
  • MSQ wording. "Which of the following are correct" means mark every correct option. Missing one gives zero for the question.

Many of these have cousins in the technical section; see how to attempt each format in the MCQ, MSQ and NAT strategy guide.

An 18-minute attempt plan

GA should take no more than about 18 minutes of the 180-minute paper. This order, from the book's time strategy, handles the quick items first:

Step Items Time
1 Grammar, vocabulary, analogy, defined operation, series (1-mark recognition items) 4 minutes
2 Quantitative word problems, worked on the rough sheet 5 minutes
3 Spatial item, with a quick sketch 2 minutes
4 Data interpretation: compute every needed value before judging any statement 3 minutes
5 Reading comprehension and para-jumble 4 minutes

If a 2-mark GA item has no plan within 90 seconds, mark it for review and move on, because 85 technical marks are waiting. Come back in the last 10 minutes. Never leave a NAT or MSQ blank at the end, since there is no negative marking. For an unresolved MCQ, answer only if you have eliminated two options.

How to prepare GA alongside CSE

GA does not need a separate block of weeks. It needs regular, short practice that fits around your technical subjects.

  1. Cover the every-year families first. Reading comprehension, grammar, percentages and ratios, data interpretation and one spatial type. These carry most of the section.
  2. Solve recent GA sections. The four-part syllabus dates from 2021, so papers from 2021 onward are the closest match to the current style.
  3. Classify every reading comprehension option. Label each as stated, inferred or beyond the passage before choosing.
  4. Sketch every spatial item. Thirty seconds on a sketch removes the mirror and rotation confusion.
  5. Attempt GA inside full mocks. Timing only becomes real when GA shares the clock with 55 technical questions. The GATE CSE mock test strategy explains how to take and review a mock, and the GATE CSE 2027 preparation strategy shows where GA fits in a full plan.

Treat GA as marks you can protect rather than marks you have to chase, and it becomes the steadiest part of your score.

Frequently asked questions

How many marks is General Aptitude in GATE CSE?

General Aptitude has 10 questions worth 15 marks: five 1-mark questions and five 2-mark questions. Every CS paper since GA was introduced in 2010 has followed this structure. The rest of the 100 marks come from Engineering Mathematics, about 13 marks, and the core computer science subjects, about 72 marks.

What is the GATE 2027 General Aptitude syllabus?

It has four parts. Verbal Aptitude covers grammar, vocabulary, reading comprehension and narrative sequencing. Quantitative Aptitude covers data interpretation, numerical computation, mensuration, statistics and probability. Analytical Aptitude covers deduction, induction, analogy and numerical reasoning. Spatial Aptitude covers shape transformations, paper folding and patterns. The text is unchanged from 2026.

Is GATE General Aptitude difficult?

Analysts have rated it the easiest section of the CS paper in recent years: easy in the 2021 to 2024 analyses, easy to moderate in 2025, and easy or easy to moderate in both 2026 shifts. Marks are usually lost to traps such as adding successive percentages or confusing a stated fact with an inference.

How much time should I spend on General Aptitude in GATE?

Aim for 15 to 18 minutes out of the 180-minute paper, targeting 13 to 15 marks. If GA takes more than 20 minutes, one item has absorbed time it did not deserve. If a 2-mark GA question has no plan within 90 seconds, mark it for review and return in the last 10 minutes.

Is there negative marking in GATE General Aptitude?

Only for MCQs. A wrong 1-mark MCQ costs one third of a mark and a wrong 2-mark MCQ costs two thirds. MSQ and NAT questions carry no negative marking. Since 2021 the GA section can include MSQ and NAT items as well as MCQs, so read the question type before answering.
