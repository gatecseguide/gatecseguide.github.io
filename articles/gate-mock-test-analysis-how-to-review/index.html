GATE Mock Test Analysis: How to Review a Mock and Plan the Next Week

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 9 Oct 20269 min read

You finish a GATE mock, see a score, and wonder what to do with it. The answer is that the score is the least useful number on the page. A proper review turns one afternoon's paper into a plan for the next seven days. This guide teaches that review step by step, for every GATE branch, with one fully worked example.

Key takeaways

  • Score the mock with GATE's own marking scheme before you read a single solution.
  • Give every wrong, skipped and slow question one row in an error log, with one cause from a fixed list of five.
  • Four numbers summarise a mock: accuracy, attempt rate, marks lost to negatives and marks per minute.
  • Split lost marks into "didn't know" and "knew but lost". The second group is the fastest to recover.
  • Each cause has its own fix, and the next seven days should go to the cause that cost the most marks.
  • Compare mocks by their rates and causes, not by the raw score, because mock difficulty changes.

The words this review uses

Every GATE paper has 65 questions for 100 marks in 3 hours, and each question is worth 1 or 2 marks. Those two facts fix the split: 30 one-mark and 35 two-mark questions, since 30+35=6530 + 35 = 6530+35=65 and 30+2×35=10030 + 2 \times 35 = 10030+2×35=100. General Aptitude is 15 of the 100 marks in every paper. Confirm the current pattern at gate2027.iitm.ac.in.

Five terms carry the rest of this guide.

  • Net marks: marks for correct answers minus the negative marks for wrong MCQs.
  • Accuracy: the share of attempted questions you got right.
  • Attempt rate: the share of the 65 questions you attempted at all.
  • Marks lost: for one wrong question, the marks it carried plus any penalty it charged.
  • Marks per minute: net marks earned in a stretch of the paper, divided by the minutes it took.

Step 1: score it exactly as GATE would

Score the paper with the answer key first, and leave the solutions closed. Reading solutions first makes every wrong answer look like a slip, and the log turns dishonest.

Type Wrong answer costs Partial credit
MCQ, 1 mark −13\displaystyle -\frac{1}{3}−31​ Not applicable
MCQ, 2 marks −23\displaystyle -\frac{2}{3}−32​ Not applicable
MSQ Nothing None: the full correct set or zero
NAT Nothing None: correct at the stated precision or zero

The scheme is the same for every GATE paper, and it is set out in the GATE exam pattern and marking scheme guide, which applies to all branches. Check the current figures on the official question paper pattern page before you rely on them.

Trap: An MSQ with one extra option ticked is simply wrong. Log it as wrong, not as "nearly right".

Step 2: build the error log

The error log is a table with one row for every question that did not earn full marks, plus any correct one that took far too long. Use the same columns every time, so that logs from different mocks can be added together.

Column What to write
Question Number in the paper
Section General Aptitude, mathematics, or the subject it belongs to
Type MCQ, MSQ or NAT
Marks 1 or 2
Outcome Correct but slow, wrong, or skipped
Cause One of the five causes below
Marks lost Marks carried plus any penalty

Each row gets exactly one cause. If two seem to fit, pick the one that happened first.

Cause What it means Which group
Concept gap You did not know the idea or how to start Didn't know
Guess You picked an answer without a method Didn't know
Calculation slip Right method, wrong arithmetic, sign or unit Knew but lost
Misread You answered a different question from the one asked Knew but lost
Time You knew it but rushed or ran out of minutes Knew but lost

Step 3: compute the four numbers

Accuracy is correct answers over attempted questions, and attempt rate is attempted questions over all 65:

Accuracy=CA×100%,Attempt rate=A65×100%\text{Accuracy} = \frac{C}{A} \times 100\%, \qquad \text{Attempt rate} = \frac{A}{65} \times 100\%Accuracy=AC​×100%,Attempt rate=65A​×100%

Here CCC is the number of correct answers and AAA the number attempted. Net marks are the positive marks PPP minus the penalty, where W1W_1W1​ and W2W_2W2​ count wrong 1-mark and 2-mark MCQs:

Net=P−13W1−23W2\text{Net} = P - \tfrac{1}{3}W_1 - \tfrac{2}{3}W_2Net=P−31​W1​−32​W2​

Remember: Score first, read solutions second. A review built on solutions you have already read hides your real causes.

Step 4: separate "didn't know" from "knew but lost"

Group the log by cause. In the example the 14 wrong answers split like this, with marks lost including penalties:

Cause Questions Marks lost

Guess 3 163≈5.33\displaystyle \frac{16}{3} \approx 5.33316​≈5.33
Calculation slip 4 253≈8.33\displaystyle \frac{25}{3} \approx 8.33325​≈8.33
Misread 2 73≈2.33\displaystyle \frac{7}{3} \approx 2.3337​≈2.33
Time 1 2

The first two rows are "didn't know": 263+163=14\displaystyle \frac{26}{3} + \frac{16}{3} = 14326​+316​=14 marks. The last three are "knew but lost": 253+73+2=383≈12.67\displaystyle \frac{25}{3} + \frac{7}{3} + 2 = \frac{38}{3} \approx 12.67325​+37​+2=338​≈12.67 marks. Without studying a single new topic, this candidate's ceiling was:

Ceiling=1363+383=1743=58\text{Ceiling} = \tfrac{136}{3} + \tfrac{38}{3} = \tfrac{174}{3} = 58Ceiling=3136​+338​=3174​=58

That gap of nearly 13 marks is the most useful number in the whole review. It is recoverable with habits, not new chapters.

In one line: "Didn't know" needs study; "knew but lost" needs practice and habits, and it is usually cheaper to fix.

Step 5: a seven-day plan for each cause

Give most of the next week to the cause with the largest marks lost. The table shows what each cause asks of you.

Cause Days 1-3 Days 4-7
Concept gap Re-learn the idea from your notes or textbook Solve fresh questions on it, timed
Guess List the topics you guessed on and study the two biggest Practise eliminating options before answering
Calculation slip Redo each slipped question cleanly One timed numerical set a day; check units on every line
Misread Rewrite each misread question in your own words Underline the quantity asked before solving, every time
Time Find where the minutes went Set a skip rule, such as 90 seconds with no plan, and drill it

Guessing deserves one line of arithmetic. A blind guess on a 1-mark MCQ has an expected value of 14(1)−34(13)=0\displaystyle \frac{1}{4}(1) - \frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = 041​(1)−43​(31​)=0. Rule out one option and it becomes 13(1)−23(13)=19\displaystyle \frac{1}{3}(1) - \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{9}31​(1)−32​(31​)=91​. So guess an MCQ only after an elimination, and always enter an answer for MSQ and NAT.

Comparing mocks without chasing the raw score

Mocks differ in difficulty, so two raw scores are not directly comparable. Track the rates and the knew-but-lost total instead. Here are three hypothetical mocks, the first being the worked example:

Mock Net Accuracy Attempt rate Negative marks Knew but lost
1 45.33 70.8% 73.8% 4.67 12.67
2 51.00 76.0% 76.9% 3.00 8.00
3, a harder paper 44.67 82.2% 69.2% 1.33 5.33

Mock 3's score fell, yet accuracy rose, negative marks fell and knew-but-lost marks more than halved since mock 1. That is a candidate improving on a tougher paper. The real exam's marks are later turned into a GATE score, as the score and normalisation guide explains, so steady habits matter more than one mock's number.

Trap: Repeating a mock you have already reviewed inflates the score. Use repeats only for timed practice, never for the trend table.

Every two or three mocks, add the logs together and sort by section and cause. A cause that tops the list twice in a row is where your next full week belongs.

Quick revision

  1. Score with the official scheme first: −13\displaystyle -\frac{1}{3}−31​ and −23\displaystyle -\frac{2}{3}−32​ for wrong MCQs, nothing for MSQ and NAT.
  2. Every wrong, skipped or slow question gets one row and exactly one cause.
  3. The five causes are concept gap, guess, calculation slip, misread and time.
  4. Accuracy is CA\displaystyle \frac{C}{A}AC​, attempt rate is A65\displaystyle \frac{A}{65}65A​, and marks lost include both the marks forgone and the penalty.
  5. Net marks plus knew-but-lost marks gives your ceiling without new study.
  6. Spend the next seven days on the cause that cost the most marks.
  7. A blind MCQ guess is worth zero on average; guess only after ruling out an option.
  8. Compare mocks by accuracy, negatives and knew-but-lost marks, not by raw score alone.

Frequently asked questions

How do I analyse a GATE mock test?

Score it first with the official marking scheme, before reading any solution. Then give every wrong, skipped and slow question a row in an error log with its section, type, marks, outcome and cause. Compute accuracy, attempt rate, marks lost to negatives and marks per minute, then plan the next seven days around the cause that cost the most marks.

What is a good accuracy in a GATE mock test?

There is no official figure. Accuracy is correct answers divided by questions attempted, and it matters because every wrong MCQ also costs a third of its marks. Rather than chase a number, watch the trend across your own mocks. Rising accuracy with falling negative marks usually means your attempt decisions are improving, even when the raw score moves around.

Should I compare my mock scores with each other?

Only with care. Mocks differ in difficulty, so a lower score on a harder paper can still be progress. Compare the rates that do not depend on the paper so much, such as accuracy, marks lost to negatives and marks you knew but lost. Those show whether your habits are improving from one mock to the next.

Is it worth guessing an MCQ in GATE?

A blind guess on a four-option MCQ has an expected value of zero under GATE marking, because the one-in-four chance of full marks is cancelled by the three-in-four chance of losing a third. Once you can rule out at least one option the expected value turns positive. MSQ and NAT carry no negative marks, so always enter an answer there.

How long should I spend reviewing one GATE mock?

Plan a separate session the day after the mock rather than reviewing straight away when you are tired. Two to three hours is a sensible starting point for a full paper with a careful error log. If a week is short, take fewer mocks and keep the full review, because an unreviewed mock gives you a score and nothing to act on.
