GATE DA Syllabus Topics Never Asked in Any Paper Yet

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 18 Nov 20267 min read

Four topic groups named in the GATE DA syllabus have never drawn a counted question: confidence intervals with the z, t and chi-squared tests, LU decomposition, linked lists, and functions of a single variable on their own. Several smaller items are also unset. This guide gives each of them a short, honest reading with a worked example, and explains why their zero says nothing about 2027.

Key takeaways

  • The four never-asked groups are named, in the same words, in the syllabus the 2026 paper was set on. Their zero is real.
  • A zero over three papers still predicts nothing. No past-paper practice will ever make you meet these topics.
  • Confidence intervals and the three tests are one formula each. LU is Gaussian elimination written down.
  • Linked lists are pointer bookkeeping and a short table of costs.
  • Smaller unset items include mergesort, linear search, partition matrices, multiple linear regression, k-fold cross-validation and divisive clustering.
  • Give each one a careful reading and a few questions, not a week of drilling.

What "never asked" means, and two ways of counting it

A topic is never asked when none of the 165 counted technical questions of 2024, 2025 and 2026 was placed on it. The GATE DA 2027 book placed every question on one topic group, using the official keys.

The book quotes the list two ways. Its summary names four groups. Its do-not-skip chapter names three, leaving out functions of a single variable, because that phrase is the setting of every calculus question rather than a topic of its own. This guide covers all four.

Confidence intervals and the three tests

A confidence interval is a range built from a sample that would contain the true mean in a stated share of repeated samples. When the population standard deviation σ\sigmaσ is known, the 95 per cent interval is the sample mean plus or minus 1.96 standard errors:

xˉ±1.96 σn\bar{x} \pm 1.96\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}xˉ±1.96n​σ​

Here xˉ\bar{x}xˉ is the sample mean and nnn the sample size.

Worked example: an interval and a z-test

Take n=36n = 36n=36 readings with xˉ=50\bar{x} = 50xˉ=50 and known σ=6\sigma = 6σ=6. The standard error is 6/36=16 / \sqrt{36} = 16/36​=1, so the 95 per cent interval is 50±1.9650 \pm 1.9650±1.96, that is from 48.04 to 51.96.

Now test whether a mean of 50 is plausible when a new sample of 36 gives xˉ=52\bar{x} = 52xˉ=52. The z statistic measures the gap in standard errors:

z=xˉ−μ0σ/n=52−506/6=2.0z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_{0}}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} = \frac{52 - 50}{6 / 6} = 2.0z=σ/n​xˉ−μ0​​=6/652−50​=2.0

For a two-sided test at 5 per cent, reject when ∣z∣>1.96\vert z \vert > 1.96∣z∣>1.96. Here 2.0>1.962.0 > 1.962.0>1.96, so reject.

The t-test is the same statistic with the sample standard deviation sss in place of σ\sigmaσ, read against a t-distribution with n−1n - 1n−1 degrees of freedom. The chi-squared test compares observed and expected counts with ∑(O−E)2/E\sum (O - E)^{2} / E∑(O−E)2/E.

Trap: Use 1.96 for a two-sided 5 per cent test and 1.645 for a one-sided one. Mixing them up is the commonest wrong answer on this topic.

LU decomposition

LU decomposition writes a square matrix as a lower triangular LLL times an upper triangular UUU. UUU is the echelon form you get from Gaussian elimination, and LLL stores the multipliers you used.

Worked example: a 2 by 2 factorisation

For A=[3164]A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 6 & 4 \end{bmatrix}A=[36​14​], subtract 2 times row 1 from row 2. The multiplier is 2 and the new row 2 is (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2):

A=[1021][3102]A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}A=[12​01​][30​12​]

Multiply back to check: the product is AAA. A bonus fact follows: det⁡A=3×2=6\det A = 3 \times 2 = 6detA=3×2=6, the product of UUU's diagonal.

If you can do elimination, LU costs ten extra minutes of reading. It exists without row swaps when every leading principal minor is non-zero.

Linked lists

A linked list is a chain of nodes, each holding a value and a reference to the next node. It gives up random access in exchange for cheap insertion at known positions.

Operation Cost
Access the k-th element O(k)O(k)O(k)
Insert at the head O(1)O(1)O(1)
Delete a node, given its predecessor O(1)O(1)O(1)
Search for a value O(n)O(n)O(n)

The question to expect, if one comes, is a short trace: a few pointer reassignments, then "what does the list hold now?" Draw boxes and arrows, and redraw after every line.

Functions of a single variable

This phrase names the setting of the whole calculus section: domain, range, composition and monotonicity of ordinary functions. No counted question was about it on its own, but every calculus question used it. Read it as revision of standard function behaviour, not as a separate chapter.

Why the zero says nothing about 2027

Three papers are 195 questions, and the syllabus has 132 phrases. Gaps are certain. A named topic can miss three papers by chance alone.

GATE 2027 is set by IIT Madras, which has never set a DA paper, as the guide to the organising institute explains. The book's revision plan predicts, and labels it as a prediction, that unexamined named topics are among the more likely places for a new question.

How to fit them into your plan

  1. Give them one dedicated week in the middle of your preparation, plus a half-day revisit in the last month.
  2. Practise the test statistics with the on-screen calculator; the virtual calculator guide helps.
  3. Expect MSQ statements on them; the marking scheme guide, common to every GATE paper, explains why one wrong tick scores zero.
  4. Check the calendar on the exam dates guide.

This guide gives the short reading. The GATE DA 2027 book writes practice questions on every never-asked topic, and says on each one that it rests on the syllabus alone.

Frequently asked questions

Which GATE DA syllabus topics have never been asked?

Across the 2024, 2025 and 2026 papers, no counted question was placed on confidence intervals with the z, t and chi-squared tests, on LU decomposition, on linked lists, or on functions of a single variable as a topic on its own. Smaller unset items include mergesort, linear search, partition matrices, multiple linear regression and k-fold cross-validation.

Can I skip GATE DA topics that have never been asked?

No. They are named in the 2027 syllabus, which was word for word the 2026 one, and three papers are too few for an absence to predict anything. Most are short, so a careful reading and a handful of practice questions cost little and cover a gap no past paper can fill.

Will confidence intervals come in GATE DA 2027?

Nobody can say. They are named in the syllabus and have drawn nothing in three papers. The book's revision plan treats unexamined named topics as among the more likely places for a new question, and labels that as a prediction. Prepare the interval and the three tests in an afternoon either way.

Why does the count of never-asked GATE DA topics differ between sources?

Because of one judgement call. The book's summary lists four groups, including functions of a single variable on their own. Its do-not-skip chapter lists three, leaving that one out because it is the setting of every calculus question rather than a separate topic. Both describe the same record.
