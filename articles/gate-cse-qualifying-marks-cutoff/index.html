GATE CSE Qualifying Marks and Cutoffs Explained

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 21 Sep 20268 min read

Two different numbers get called "the GATE cutoff", and mixing them up wastes a lot of worry. The qualifying marks are set by the organising institute and decide who gets a scorecard. The admission cutoff is set later by each institute or recruiter, and it is much higher. This guide explains both, prints the official formula, and gives past CS qualifying marks with the year and source for each.

Key takeaways

  • Qualifying marks are computed from the paper, not fixed in advance. The official General-category formula is max(25, min(40, μ + σ)).
  • OBC-NCL and EWS qualifying marks are 9/10 of the General value. SC, ST and PwD qualifying marks are 2/3 of it.
  • Qualifying gets you a scorecard, valid for three years. It does not get you a seat or a job.
  • Admission cutoffs are published by each institute after its own counselling round, and PSU shortlisting cutoffs come from each recruitment notification.
  • Past CS qualifying marks are history, not a forecast. The 2027 number is computed afresh from the 2027 paper.

What "qualifying marks" actually means

After the GATE 2027 papers are evaluated, the organising institute works out a threshold for each test paper. Candidates at or above that threshold qualify and can download a scorecard. Candidates below it do not.

The brochure states the rule directly. For the General category in each subject:

Qualifying marks for GENERAL category = max(25, min(40, μ + σ))

Here μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation of the marks of all candidates who appeared for that test paper. The other categories follow fixed ratios:

  • OBC-NCL and EWS: (9/10) × the General qualifying marks
  • SC, ST and PwD: (2/3) × the General qualifying marks

Read the formula slowly and three things fall out. First, the threshold can never be below 25 or above 40, whatever happens to the paper. Second, it moves with the cohort: a paper that a lot of people did well on pushes μ + σ up. Third, nobody can announce it before the exam, because μ and σ do not exist until everybody has written.

That same qualifying mark, written as Mq, then feeds the score formula. It is the mark that maps to a GATE score of 350. The mechanics of that conversion are covered in how the GATE score is calculated and normalised.

Past CS qualifying marks, by year

These are the published qualifying marks for the CS (Computer Science and Information Technology) paper, taken from the official cut-off page of each year's organising institute. They are a record of what happened, not a prediction of 2027.

Year Organising institute General OBC-NCL / EWS SC / ST / PwD CS candidates appeared
2024 IISc Bengaluru 27.6 24.8 18.4 1,23,967
2025 IIT Roorkee 29.2 26.2 19.4 1,70,825
2026 IIT Guwahati 30.0 27.0 20.0 2,11,020

The CS paper has grown quickly: roughly 1.24 lakh candidates appeared in 2024 and about 2.11 lakh in 2026. Do not read the rising qualifying marks as a trend line you can extend. The formula is driven by μ and σ of a specific paper, so a harder 2027 paper would push the number down even with more candidates. Treat 25 as the floor the brochure guarantees and nothing more.

Qualifying is the floor, not the target

Here is the part that changes how you prepare. Clearing roughly 30 marks out of 100 gets you a scorecard. It does not get you into a department. Seats and jobs are handed out in rank order, and the queue for CS is one of the longest in GATE.

So set your target from the other end. Decide which programmes or recruiters you are actually aiming at, look at the closing data those bodies publish after each cycle, and work backwards to a mark. Preparing to "just qualify" is preparing for a scorecard you will not be able to use.

What the scorecard does give you is time. It is valid for three years from the announcement of results, so a GATE 2027 score can be used in admission and recruitment cycles beyond 2027, subject to whatever each body accepts. For GATE 2027, the scorecard is free to download during the announced window; after that a fee of ₹500 per test paper applies, and from 1 January 2028 onward scorecards will not be issued.

Admission cutoffs are a different animal

An admission cutoff is the score or rank at which a particular programme stopped admitting in a particular round. It is produced by a counselling process, not by the organising institute, and it is specific to one institute, one specialisation, one category and one round.

That means four things vary at once:

What varies Why it moves
Institute and department Reputation, location and intake differ
Specialisation A general CS M.Tech and a narrow specialisation fill differently
Category Each category has its own closing position
Round Later rounds close lower as candidates accept elsewhere

Because of that, no honest article can tell you "the cutoff for institute X is Y". The numbers you need are published by the counselling bodies themselves after each round. M.Tech admission after GATE CSE through COAP and CCMT explains which portal covers which institutes and where those round-wise figures appear.

Public sector recruitment works the same way. Each organisation sets its own shortlisting cutoff in its own notification, with its own discipline mapping and its own weightage between GATE score and interview. PSU recruitment through GATE CSE covers how to read those notifications.

Why your marks and your score are not the same number

Your scorecard shows two numbers: marks out of 100 and a GATE score out of 1000. They are not interchangeable, and admission bodies mostly care about the second one.

The score is produced by mapping your marks onto a scale anchored at two points: the qualifying mark becomes 350 and the mean of the top performers becomes 900. If the paper is hard and the top scores are low, a given mark converts into a higher score than it would in an easy year. This is deliberate — it is what makes scores comparable across years and across the multiple sessions the CS paper is usually split into.

The official GATE FAQ also notes that a candidate can end up with a negative GATE score even with positive marks, which is exactly what the mapping does to marks well below the qualifying threshold. Nothing has gone wrong when that happens.

Turning this into a preparation plan

If "clear the qualifying mark" is not the goal, what is? Three habits do most of the work.

  1. Count marks by subject, not by chapter. Over the last many CS papers the weight is heavily concentrated in a handful of subjects. GATE CSE subject-wise weightage lays out where the marks have historically come from, so your hours can follow the marks.
  2. Protect the free 15. General Aptitude carries 15 marks in every test paper and rewards practice more than talent. Leaving it to exam day is a quiet way to lose the margin between qualifying and competing.
  3. Score your mocks against a real target. Track your marks out of 100 across full-length attempts, not topic quizzes. A steady number that clears your target with room to spare is the only useful evidence you have before February.

For the ordering of subjects and the weekly structure around all this, the GATE CSE 2027 preparation strategy sets out a twenty-week plan. What to check and when

  • Before the exam: confirm the pattern and marking scheme on gate2027.iitm.ac.in. Nothing about qualifying marks is announced in advance, so ignore any page claiming to know the 2027 number.
  • With the result: the organising institute publishes the qualifying marks for every paper alongside the result. That is the only authoritative source for the 2027 figure.
  • After the result: admission and recruitment cutoffs appear on the counselling portals and in individual notifications, round by round.

Frequently asked questions

What are the qualifying marks for GATE CSE?

The organising institute computes them from the paper itself. For the General category the brochure formula is max(25, min(40, mean plus standard deviation)) of all candidates who appeared. OBC-NCL and EWS get nine-tenths of that value and SC, ST and PwD get two-thirds. The exact number is published with the result each year.

Is the GATE cutoff the same as the qualifying marks?

No. Qualifying marks are the threshold set by the organising institute for getting a scorecard. Admission cutoffs are closing scores or ranks that each IIT, NIT or other institute publishes after its own counselling round, and they are far higher. A PSU sets its own shortlisting cutoff in its recruitment notification.

What was the GATE CS qualifying mark in 2026?

The official GATE 2026 cut-off page published by IIT Guwahati lists 30 for General, 27 for OBC-NCL and EWS, and 20 for SC, ST and PwD in the CS paper. That is history for one year only. Because the formula depends on how candidates performed, the 2027 value will be computed afresh.

Does qualifying in GATE guarantee admission or a job?

No. The official GATE FAQ states plainly that qualifying in GATE does not guarantee admission, scholarship or employment, and that GATE qualification does not assure a job in a public sector undertaking. Qualifying only makes you eligible to apply where a valid GATE score is required.

How long is a GATE CSE scorecard valid?

Three years from the date the results are announced. For GATE 2027 the scorecard can be downloaded free during the announced window, after which a fee of 500 rupees per paper applies, and no scorecard is issued from 1 January 2028 onward. Confirm the current rule on the official website.

How many marks should I target in GATE CSE?

Aim well above the qualifying mark, because admission and recruitment happen on rank, not on qualifying. Qualifying in recent CS papers sat around the high twenties, while seats at the most competitive departments close at scores many times harder to reach. Set your target from the closing data of the programmes you actually want.
