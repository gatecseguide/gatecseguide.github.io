GATE CSE 6-Month Study Plan (24 Weeks), Month by Month

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 22 Sep 20268 min read

A six-month GATE CSE plan is 24 weeks, and its defining feature is not its length but its rhythm: three weeks of new work followed by one consolidation week, repeated six times. That rhythm is what stops the usual failure of long plans, where the first two months are revised for the first time in February. This article lays out the 24 weeks month by month, and shows how to fit them into the time actually left before GATE 2027.

Key takeaways

  • Every month runs three study weeks plus one consolidation week; previous year questions are solved monthly, not saved for a single phase.
  • Each month pairs one heavy subject with one light, formula-stable subject, so no subject is orphaned at the end.
  • The month ends with a 30-question, 90-minute checkpoint paper covering only that month's subjects and General Aptitude.
  • Full 180-minute mocks begin in Month 5, after every subject has been seen once.
  • Only about 20 weeks remain to 6 February 2027, so the 24-week shape has to lose four weeks; the compression table below says which.

How much time is really left

GATE 2027 runs on 6–7, 13–14 and 20–21 February 2027, with results on 19 March 2027. From Monday 21 September 2026 there are about 20 weeks to the first examination day, or about 22 if your paper falls on 20 or 21 February. The paper is 3 hours, 65 questions, 100 marks, with 15 marks of General Aptitude, about 13 of Engineering Mathematics and about 72 of core CS. Dates and the slot for each paper are announced by the organising institute, so confirm them at gate2027.iitm.ac.in.

A true 24-week run for this cycle would have begun in late August. If you are reading this in late September, use the compression table at the end. If you are reading it for a later GATE window, start six months and one week before your examination date and run the plan at full length.

The monthly rhythm

Week of the month What it is for Output
1 Heavy subject: the highest-frequency concepts, theory then practice Notes and worked examples for each concept
2 Heavy subject continued, plus the light subject's core Timed practice at the target speed
3 Light subject finished; second-tier topics of both subjects covered once Formula page for both subjects
4 Consolidation: previous year questions for both subjects, error log, checkpoint paper Subject scorecard and an updated error log

Assume 25 to 30 hours a week over six days, with 20 minutes of General Aptitude daily throughout all six months rather than a block anywhere. General Aptitude is 15 marks and is rated easy in every recent paper, so it earns its daily slot rather than a block anywhere in the six months.

The consolidation week is what makes this plan different from the 20-week preparation strategy, which gathers all previous year practice into a single phase in the middle. Here you solve them while the theory is a week old, which is when the questions teach the most and when your error log is still honest.

The six months

Month Heavy subject Light subject Consolidation week focus
1 Engineering Mathematics: probability, Bayes, distributions, eigenvalues, rank and systems Discrete Mathematics: logic, counting, relations, graphs Mathematics questions 2014–2026; checkpoint paper 1
2 Programming and Data Structures: C semantics, pointers, 2-D arrays, recursion, BST, heaps, traversals Digital Logic: Boolean algebra, K-maps, 2's complement, IEEE-754 Tracing under time; checkpoint paper 2
3 Algorithms: asymptotics, recurrences, sorting and hashing counts, greedy, dynamic programming, graph algorithms Compiler Design: FIRST/FOLLOW, LL(1), LR states, syntax-directed translation Graph and dynamic programming questions; checkpoint paper 3
4 Operating Systems: scheduling, synchronization, paging and TLB, page replacement, deadlock Computer Organization: cache arithmetic, AMAT, pipeline timing and hazards Combined paging-plus-TLB and cache-plus-pipeline items; checkpoint paper 4
5 Computer Networks on the 2027 list, plus Databases Theory of Computation: classification, closure, decidability Full-syllabus previous year sweep; first two full mocks
6 No new theory No new theory Mocks at one or two a week, error log, last-minute sheet

Why the subjects are paired this way

The pairing rule is the plan's second structural idea. Compiler Design and Digital Logic are low-weight subjects, roughly 4 to 7 marks each in recent papers, but they are the most formula-stable material in the syllabus and give the best marks per hour of preparation. In plans that run strictly in order of weightage they land at the end and get abandoned. Pairing each of them with a heavy subject protects them. The subject-wise weightage article shows the marks history that this ordering is built on.

Within a month, the heavy subject gets about two-thirds of the hours. Mathematics comes first because it is needed by Algorithms, Databases and Theory of Computation later; Programming and Data Structures comes second because it is the largest generator of numerical answer type questions and takes the longest to become fast at.

The checkpoint paper

At the end of each consolidation week, sit a 30-question paper in 90 minutes covering only the subjects of that month plus General Aptitude, scored with the official marking scheme. It is not a mock; its job is to answer one question honestly: can you still do last month's work under time?

Record three numbers each month in a single table: marks, accuracy on the questions you attempted, and the count of questions you could not start. The third number is the one that predicts trouble. If it grows for two months in a row you are covering material without reaching usable speed, and the fix is to narrow what you study rather than to speed up your reading.

Month 5 and Month 6: from coverage to scoring

Month 5 is the turn. In its first three weeks you finish the last subjects and take your first two full 180-minute mocks, each in a single sitting at the clock time of your likely slot. From the consolidation week onwards, the error log rather than the syllabus decides what you revise.

Month 6 carries no new theory at all. Mocks run at one or two a week, each followed the next day by a written analysis of every wrong, skipped and slow question. Between mocks, work on whatever the analysis exposes: trap drills, formula recall, pacing. The mock test strategy guide covers scoring and analysis step by step. The last ten days are revision only, as set out in the last-month revision plan.

Compressing 24 weeks into the 20 that are left

If you are starting on or near 21 September 2026, run the plan with these four weeks removed. Cut in this order and no other.

Change Weeks saved What it costs
Month 1 shortened to two weeks: probability, Bayes, distributions, eigenvalues and logic only 2 Counting, recurrences and graph theory move into Month 3's consolidation week
Month 2 and Month 3 consolidation weeks cut to four days 1 Older previous year questions are skipped; 2014–2026 only
Month 4's light subject reduced to its highest-frequency concepts 0.5 Computer Organization second-tier topics move to Month 6
Month 5's Theory of Computation reduced to classification, closure and decidability 0.5 Pumping lemma proofs and pushdown automata tracing are dropped

What is never cut: the checkpoint papers, the Month 6 mocks and the final ten days. If more than four weeks need to go, the plan is the wrong shape for your remaining time, and the two-month plan is the better starting point.

A realistic weekly timetable

Day Morning Evening
Monday to Friday 20 minutes General Aptitude, then 2 hours on the month's heavy subject 2 hours of practice questions on the morning's concept
Saturday 3 hours on the light subject 1 hour writing the week's formula page and error log
Sunday Rest, or the checkpoint paper in consolidation weeks Rest

Six study days rather than seven is deliberate over six months. A plan that uses every Sunday for twenty-four weeks is not kept; a plan with one protected rest day usually is.

Frequently asked questions

Is 6 months enough to prepare for GATE CSE?

Six months at 25 to 30 hours a week is enough to cover the whole syllabus once, solve the previous year questions topic by topic and take a full set of mock tests. It is more comfortable than a twenty-week run because each month ends with a consolidation week, so nothing is left to be revised for the first time in February.

How many hours a day for a 6 month GATE plan?

About four to five hours a day across six days, which is 25 to 30 hours a week. Three weeks of each month are new theory and practice; the fourth is consolidation, where the hours go to previous year questions, the error log and a checkpoint test instead of new material.

How do I fit a 6-month plan into the time left for GATE 2027?

About twenty weeks remain between 21 September 2026 and 6 February 2027, so the 24-week shape has to lose four weeks. Fold Month 1 into two weeks by covering probability and linear algebra only, and shorten the Month 2 and Month 3 consolidation weeks to four days each. Keep Months 5 and 6 at full length.

Which subjects should be studied together in a GATE CSE plan?

Pair one heavy subject with one light, formula-stable subject each month. Programming and Data Structures with Digital Logic, Algorithms with Compiler Design, and Operating Systems with Computer Organization work well. The pairing stops the low-weight subjects from being pushed to the end, where they are usually abandoned.

When should mock tests start in a 6-month GATE plan?

Take a 30-question checkpoint paper at the end of every month from Month 1, and start full 180-minute mocks in Month 5 once every subject has been covered once. Month 6 carries the remaining mocks at one or two a week, each followed by a full analysis day.
