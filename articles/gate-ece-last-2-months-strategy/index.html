GATE ECE Last 2 Months Strategy: What to Do in the Final 8 Weeks

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 1 Oct 20268 min read

The last two months before GATE ECE are for turning what you have studied into marks you can collect under a clock, not for adding topics. This plan is for a candidate who has already been through the syllabus once. It uses the evidence from five EC papers counted question by question (2010, 2019, 2020, 2025 and 2026) to decide what to revise, how often to sit a mock and what to leave alone.

If you are starting eight weeks out with little done, this is the wrong plan. You need a first pass, not a revision pass, and the two are built differently.

Key takeaways

  • Eight weeks is enough for one revision pass, six to eight full mocks with proper reviews, and a quiet final fortnight. It is not enough to learn new sections.
  • On the five counted papers, adding each technical section's lowest showing gives 65 of the 85 technical marks. Most of the paper is ground you have already covered, so revision is where the marks are.
  • A mock costs five hours: three sitting it and two reviewing it. Scoring a mock and skipping the review wastes most of those hours.
  • Practise each section in its own format mix. Communications was half multiple choice across the counted papers, Networks more than three-quarters.
  • Give the 2027 syllabus additions a small fixed budget and stop there. Give deleted material nothing.

Why this stage is about consolidation

The counted table gives each technical section a range as well as a mean. Networks, Signals and Systems ran from 13 to 21 marks, Engineering Mathematics 9 to 15, Analog Circuits 9 to 13, Communications 9 to 12, Digital Circuits 6 to 13, Electronic Devices 7 to 11, Control Systems 6 to 10 and Electromagnetics 6 to 9. The full table is in the subject-wise weightage guide.

Add the lowest figure of each: 13+9+9+9+6+7+6+6=6513 + 9 + 9 + 9 + 6 + 7 + 6 + 6 = 6513+9+9+9+6+7+6+6=65. Even if every section came in at its worst counted showing, 65 of the 85 technical marks would still sit in the eight sections you have already studied. The book reads this as about 20 technical marks genuinely in play from paper to paper. The rest is familiar ground, and familiar ground is lost to slips, slow setups and blank numerical answers, not to missing knowledge.

There is a second reason. Thirty-five of the 65 questions are worth 2 marks and together carry 70 of the 100 marks. Those are the questions where a sign error or a wrong unit costs most, and they are what a revision pass protects.

The eight weeks at a glance

The hours below apply the book's own rates to a candidate who has already done the first pass. A revision hour is priced at a third of a first-pass hour, because revision means reproducing a result from blank paper and working two questions, not re-reading. On that rate the book's full technical revision pass comes to about 57 hours. The split across weeks is judgement.

Weeks Main job Mocks Rough hours
1–2 Revision pass on the 17 concepts found in four or five counted papers (about 25 hours), then the 23 found in three (about 18 hours) 1, at the end of week 2 50
3–4 Repair what the first mock found; one-line revision of the 38 concepts found in two papers (about 9 hours); format drills 2 40
5–6 One mock a week with its review; a fixed budget for the 2027 additions 2 40
7–8 A second fast pass on the 17 from your own notes; timed General Aptitude; the last mock, with nothing after it 2 35

That is seven mocks and roughly 165 hours, about 20 a week. If you have more time, spend it in the repair rows, not on extra mocks. The 17 recurring concepts, with the questions behind each, are listed in the GATE ECE important topics guide.

How to review a mock

A full sitting leaves roughly 20 to 25 questions you got wrong or got right slowly. At five minutes each, re-deriving the setup rather than re-reading a chapter, that is about two hours. Sort every one into a short error log with four columns:

  1. Concept — you did not know the idea. Rare at this stage, and the only kind that justifies opening a chapter.
  2. Setup — you knew the idea but chose the wrong starting equation or model.
  3. Execution — arithmetic, sign, unit or calculator slips.
  4. Format — a numerical-answer or multiple-select question left blank, or a multiple-select question stopped at the first correct option.

A section that loses marks in two mocks running goes back on the list for the following week. A section that loses marks once was probably a bad half-hour. The book's own mock papers are built to the counted shape of the paper, but nobody has sat them as an exam, so a score on one shows where you are thin; it does not predict your GATE score.

Practise each section in its own format

Pooled across the five counted papers, the share of multiple-choice questions varied widely by section: Communications 50.0 per cent, Analog Circuits 55.9, Engineering Mathematics 58.5, Electromagnetics 60.0, Electronic Devices 66.7, Digital Circuits 68.8, Control Systems 70.8 and Networks 76.8. General Aptitude was all multiple choice.

In the last two months, match your drills to those mixes. In Communications and Analog Circuits, work questions to a number with no options to lean on, and time the calculator steps with the virtual calculator guide. In Networks, practise elimination: a wrong multiple-choice answer costs a third of the question's marks, and removing one option before guessing turns a zero-value guess into a positive one.

One habit is worth checking on every mock script: zero blank numerical-answer and zero blank multiple-select questions. Neither carries negative marking. The 2025 paper had 26 such questions and the 2026 paper 23. A blank and a wrong answer score the same there, so a blank is simply a mark you did not try for.

The 2027 additions: a fixed budget, then stop

The 2027 syllabus changed in four places against 2026. FIR and IIR filter design is newly named in Networks, Signals and Systems. PID control replaces the lag and lead-lag compensation lines in Control Systems. Correlation and regression enter Engineering Mathematics while nonlinear first-order differential equations leave. Energy-band formation and MOSFET scaling enter Electronic Devices.

Two of these have a counted precedent: filter design in 2019 Q39 and Q54, and correlation in 2026 Q38. Regression, PID control and the energy-band and scaling pair have none. Give the whole group a few scheduled hours in weeks 5–6, learn the standard form of each, and move on. The detail is in the 2027 syllabus changes guide.

The deleted material gets nothing. 2019 Q12 and 2020 Q37 are counted questions on nonlinear first-order equations, and that topic is no longer in the syllabus. If an old drill book still has the chapter, skip it.

The final fortnight

  • No new topics. Finish what is half-learned. That is where the marks closest to you are.
  • Work from your own notes and a revision sheet. The test for readiness is reading your whole sheet in about ninety minutes with almost nothing on it you do not recognise.
  • General Aptitude every day. Fifteen marks in every counted paper, all multiple choice. Ten timed questions a day keeps the speed.
  • The last mock is the last one. Sit it a few days before your paper and do nothing more in the way of full tests. Use the remaining days for the review and the sheet.
  • Sort the logistics early. Admit card, ID and reporting time are covered in the admit card and exam day rules guide. GATE 2027 is scheduled for 6–7, 13–14 and 20–21 February 2027. Take your paper's day from the official paper-wise schedule and check the current notification on the official site, as dates can change.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do in the last 2 months before GATE ECE?

If you have already covered the syllabus once, stop adding topics and start converting. Do one revision pass on the concepts that recur in four or five of the counted papers, then sit a full mock every week and spend two hours reviewing each one. Keep General Aptitude in a daily slot and use the final fortnight for your notes and one last mock.

How many mock tests should I take in the last two months of GATE ECE?

Six to eight is a sensible number, one a week from the end of the second week. Each costs about five hours once you count a two-hour review, so ten mocks and a proper revision pass rarely fit into eight weeks. Fewer mocks with full reviews teach more than many mocks you only score.

Should I start a new topic in the last month of GATE ECE preparation?

Only if it is one of the small 2027 additions you have deliberately left for a fixed hour or two. Otherwise no. In the final fortnight especially, a half-learned topic you already know is worth more than a new one, because the marks nearest to you are the ones you have almost got.

Is it too late to improve my GATE ECE score with 8 weeks left?

No, provided you have studied the syllabus once. Across the five counted papers, adding each technical section's lowest showing gives 65 of the 85 technical marks, so most of the paper is familiar ground. Eight weeks of revision and reviewed mocks is time spent making that ground reliable, which is where most lost marks come from.

How should I revise General Aptitude in the last two months?

Give it a short slot every day rather than a block at the end. It was exactly 15 marks, all multiple choice, in every counted paper, so speed and clean elimination matter more than new material. Timed runs of ten questions in the last fortnight are the most useful form of practice.
