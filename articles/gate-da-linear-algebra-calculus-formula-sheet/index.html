GATE DA Linear Algebra and Calculus Formula Sheet with Traps

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 4 Oct 20267 min read

GATE DA linear algebra rests on a few results about rank, eigenvalues, projections and the singular value decomposition. Calculus and optimisation rest on limits, Taylor series and the tests for maxima and minima. This sheet gives each formula with its condition, a one-line example of my own and the trap beside it, all checked against the GATE DA 2027 book.

Key takeaways

  • Rank plus nullity equals the number of columns, and rank alone decides how many solutions Ax=bAx = bAx=b has.
  • Eigenvalues sum to the trace and multiply to the determinant; a polynomial in AAA has the same polynomial in λ\lambdaλ.
  • Projection and idempotent matrices have eigenvalues 0 and 1 only, so their rank equals their trace.
  • The squared singular values of AAA are the eigenvalues of A⊤AA^\top AA⊤A.
  • Differentiable implies continuous, never the reverse.
  • On a closed interval, the extremes are at the end points or the interior critical points: compare the values.

The terms this sheet uses

The rank of a matrix is the number of independent columns, which equals the number of independent rows. The nullity is the dimension of the set of solutions of Ax=0Ax = 0Ax=0. An eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ of AAA is a number with Av=λvAv = \lambda vAv=λv for some non-zero vector vvv, its eigenvector.

The trace, tr⁡A\operatorname{tr} AtrA, is the sum of the diagonal. A⊤A^\topA⊤ is the transpose. A stationary point of fff is where f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0f′(x)=0. ln⁡\lnln is the natural logarithm, and η\etaη is the step size, or learning rate, of gradient descent.

Frequently asked questions

What is the relation between eigenvalues, trace and determinant?

The sum of the eigenvalues of a square matrix equals its trace, and their product equals its determinant, counting repeated eigenvalues. For a 2×22 \times 22×2 matrix this gives the characteristic equation λ2−(tr⁡A)λ+det⁡A=0\lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr} A)\lambda + \det A = 0λ2−(trA)λ+detA=0. A zero eigenvalue therefore means a zero determinant, so the matrix is singular.

What is the formula for a projection matrix?

The projection onto the column space of AAA, whose columns are independent, is P=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤P = A(A^\top A)^{-1}A^\topP=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤. Onto a single unit vector uuu it is uu⊤uu^\topuu⊤. Every projection matrix is symmetric and idempotent, so P2=PP^2 = PP2=P, its eigenvalues are only 0 and 1, and its rank equals its trace.

How do you check whether a matrix is positive definite?

A symmetric matrix is positive definite exactly when every eigenvalue is positive, or equivalently when every leading principal minor is positive. For positive semidefinite you need every eigenvalue to be non-negative. Checking only that the leading minors are non-negative is not enough for semidefiniteness, which is a classic trap.

When does gradient descent converge on a quadratic?

For f(x)=ax2f(x) = ax^2f(x)=ax2 with a>0a > 0a>0, the update x←x−ηf′(x)x \leftarrow x - \eta f'(x)x←x−ηf′(x) multiplies xxx by 1−2aη1 - 2a\eta1−2aη at each step. It converges exactly when 0<η<1a\displaystyle 0 < \eta < \frac{1}{a}0<η<a1​. At η=12a\displaystyle \eta = \frac{1}{2a}η=2a1​ it reaches the minimum in one step, and beyond 1a\displaystyle \frac{1}{a}a1​ the iterates grow without bound.

Does continuity imply differentiability?

No. Differentiability implies continuity, but not the other way round. The functions ∣x∣\lvert x \rvert∣x∣ and ReLU, max⁡(x,0)\max(x, 0)max(x,0), are continuous at 0 but have a corner there, so they are not differentiable at 0. For a piecewise function, match the values at the join for continuity, and also match the one-sided derivatives for differentiability.
