Linear Algebra Questions in GATE CSE: Eigenvalues to LU

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 1 Oct 20266 min read

Linear algebra gives GATE CSE its single most dependable question. Eigenvalue identities score 94.0 out of 100 in the book's evidence model, the joint highest in the mathematics section, and the reconstructed record puts an eigenvalue item in essentially every paper since 2003. Four question types carry the topic, and each one is a short computation rather than a proof.

Key takeaways

  • The syllabus names matrices, determinants, systems of linear equations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and LU decomposition. Nothing else is in scope.
  • Eigenvalue questions are solved with trace and determinant identities, not with characteristic polynomials.
  • Rank-based reasoning has replaced mechanical Gaussian elimination in recent papers.
  • Determinant questions test properties. Only products and polynomials of one matrix behave predictably; sums do not.
  • LU decomposition scores 54.5 and appears rarely, but it is explicitly named and takes an evening to prepare.

Type 1: eigenvalue identities

Almost no GATE eigenvalue question wants you to expand a characteristic polynomial. What they want is a derived quantity, and the identities give it in seconds.

Memorise six facts. The sum of the eigenvalues is the trace and their product is the determinant. If AAA has eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ, then:

∑iλi=tr⁡(A),∏iλi=det⁡AAk  has eigenvalue  λkA−1  has eigenvalue  1λA+cI  has eigenvalue  λ+cp(A)  has eigenvalue  p(λ)\begin{aligned} \sum_i \lambda_i &= \operatorname{tr}(A), \qquad \prod_i \lambda_i = \det A \\ A^k &\;\text{has eigenvalue}\; \lambda^k \\ A^{-1} &\;\text{has eigenvalue}\; \frac{1}{\lambda} \\ A + cI &\;\text{has eigenvalue}\; \lambda + c \\ p(A) &\;\text{has eigenvalue}\; p(\lambda) \end{aligned}i∑​λi​AkA−1A+cIp(A)​=tr(A),i∏​λi​=detAhas eigenvalueλkhas eigenvalueλ1​has eigenvalueλ+chas eigenvaluep(λ)​

Triangular matrices carry their eigenvalues on the diagonal.

Worked example: a 3×33 \times 33×3 matrix AAA has eigenvalues 1, 2 and 4. What is det⁡(A2+3I)\det(A^2 + 3I)det(A2+3I)? The eigenvalues of that combination are λ2+3\lambda^2 + 3λ2+3:

λ2+3=1+3,  4+3,  16+3=4,  7,  19det⁡(A2+3I)=4×7×19=532\begin{aligned} \lambda^2 + 3 &= 1 + 3,\; 4 + 3,\; 16 + 3 = 4,\; 7,\; 19 \\ \det(A^2 + 3I) &= 4 \times 7 \times 19 = 532 \end{aligned}λ2+3det(A2+3I)​=1+3,4+3,16+3=4,7,19=4×7×19=532​

For 2×22 \times 22×2 matrices the reverse route is just as common. Given a trace of 7 and a determinant of 12, the eigenvalues solve λ2−7λ+12=0\lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 12 = 0λ2−7λ+12=0, a quadratic with sum 7 and product 12, so λ=3\lambda = 3λ=3 and 444. No elimination required.

Special matrices give their eigenvalues away by type: orthogonal matrices have eigenvalues with ∣λ∣=1\lvert \lambda \rvert = 1∣λ∣=1, idempotent matrices (A2=AA^2 = AA2=A) have only 0 and 1, nilpotent matrices have only 0, and involutory matrices (A2=IA^2 = IA2=I) have only ±1\pm 1±1. Real symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors, which is the most quoted statement in multiple-select items on this topic.

Type 2: rank, nullity and systems of equations

The pattern that has survived two decades is the parameter question: for which values of a parameter does a 3×33 \times 33×3 system have a unique solution, infinitely many, or none? Compare the rank of the coefficient matrix with the rank of the augmented matrix.

Take the system

x+y+z=6x+2y+3z=10x+2y+az=b\begin{aligned} x + y + z &= 6 \\ x + 2y + 3z &= 10 \\ x + 2y + az &= b \end{aligned}x+y+zx+2y+3zx+2y+az​=6=10=b​

Subtracting the first row from the other two gives y+2z=4y + 2z = 4y+2z=4 and y+(a−1)z=b−6y + (a - 1)z = b - 6y+(a−1)z=b−6. Subtracting again gives (a−3)z=b−10(a - 3)z = b - 10(a−3)z=b−10. So the system has a unique solution whenever a≠3a \ne 3a=3, infinitely many solutions when a=3a = 3a=3 and b=10b = 10b=10, and no solution when a=3a = 3a=3 and b≠10b \ne 10b=10.

The trap is stopping at the determinant. A zero determinant only tells you that the unique case has failed; the augmented rank decides between infinitely many and none.

Recent papers have moved toward rank facts rather than elimination. The two worth carrying are rank⁡(AAT)=rank⁡(A)\operatorname{rank}(AA^T) = \operatorname{rank}(A)rank(AAT)=rank(A) and nullity(A)=n−rank⁡(A)\text{nullity}(A) = n - \operatorname{rank}(A)nullity(A)=n−rank(A), where nnn is the number of columns. Homogeneous systems always have the zero solution and have exactly that many independent solutions.

Type 3: determinants and matrix properties

Determinant questions test properties rather than arithmetic. Long expansions of 3×33 \times 33×3 and 4×44 \times 44×4 matrices have largely disappeared from the recent papers, replaced by property-based reasoning that a multiple-select question can grade.

The list that covers nearly every past item, for an n×nn \times nn×n matrix AAA:

det⁡(kA)=kndet⁡Adet⁡(AB)=det⁡A⋅det⁡Bdet⁡(AT)=det⁡Adet⁡(A−1)=1det⁡Adet⁡(row swap of A)=−det⁡Adet⁡(adj⁡A)=(det⁡A)n−1\begin{aligned} \det(kA) &= k^n \det A \\ \det(AB) &= \det A \cdot \det B \\ \det(A^T) &= \det A \\ \det(A^{-1}) &= \frac{1}{\det A} \\ \det(\text{row swap of } A) &= -\det A \\ \det(\operatorname{adj} A) &= (\det A)^{n-1} \end{aligned}det(kA)det(AB)det(AT)det(A−1)det(row swap of A)det(adjA)​=kndetA=detA⋅detB=detA=detA1​=−detA=(detA)n−1​

For example, if a 4×44 \times 44×4 matrix has determinant 3, then det⁡(2A)=24×3=48\det(2A) = 2^4 \times 3 = 48det(2A)=24×3=48. Answering 6 is the standard slip.

The counterpart trap is worth stating separately because it appears in both determinant and eigenvalue items: det⁡(A+B)≠det⁡A+det⁡B\det(A + B) \ne \det A + \det Bdet(A+B)=detA+detB in general, and the eigenvalues of A+BA + BA+B are not the sums of their eigenvalues. Only operations on a single matrix behave.

Cayley-Hamilton is the bridge between the two types. Every matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation, so for a 2×22 \times 22×2 matrix with trace ttt and determinant ddd, A2=tA−dIA^2 = tA - dIA2=tA−dI. That rearranges into an inverse expressed as a polynomial in the matrix, which is exactly what the paper asks for when it wants a matrix power or inverse without elimination.

Type 4: LU decomposition

LU decomposition is the clearest case in this subject of a topic that is explicitly named but rarely asked. It sits in the Moderate band at 54.5 because its history is thin and its variants are few, not because it is hard.

What has been asked is narrow: an entry of LLL or UUU for a small matrix, the existence condition, or the determinant read off the diagonal of UUU. Take the 2×22 \times 22×2 matrix with first row 2, 3 and second row 4, 11. Under the Doolittle convention, where LLL has ones on its diagonal, the multiplier is 4/2=24/2 = 24/2=2:

[23411]=[1021][2305]\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 11 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}[24​311​]=[12​01​][20​35​]

The determinant is the product of the diagonal of UUU:

det⁡A=2×5=10=2×11−3×4\det A = 2 \times 5 = 10 = 2 \times 11 - 3 \times 4detA=2×5=10=2×11−3×4

The one thing to fix in your notes is the convention. Doolittle puts the unit diagonal on LLL, Crout puts it on UUU, and a question that does not say which one it wants will tell you through the entries it gives you.

Where linear algebra fits in your plan

Linear algebra is part of the roughly 13 marks the official pattern page allots to Engineering Mathematics, shared with discrete mathematics, calculus and probability. Analyst compilations separating the two halves between 2016 and 2026 put the linear algebra, calculus and probability group between 4 and 14 marks, with a median near 6. Those figures are approximate and differ by about 2 to 3 marks between sources.

Practically, this means one eigenvalue item and one rank or systems item in most shifts. Prepare eigenvalues to the point of reflex, rank conditions to the point of a written checklist, determinant properties as a list you can recite, and LU to the point of recognising the convention. Frequently asked questions

Which linear algebra topic is most important for GATE CSE?

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors. In the book's eight-factor model the topic scores 94.0 out of 100, the joint highest in the mathematics section, and the reconstructed record places an eigenvalue item in essentially every paper from 2003 to 2026. Most of these are numeric answer type and can be finished in about a minute with the trace and determinant shortcuts.

Is LU decomposition asked in GATE CSE?

Rarely, but it is named in the official syllabus. The reconstruction shows only about five appearances, around 2015, 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2024, several of them uncertain. It scores 54.5, in the Moderate band. The honest reading is that it is cheap to prepare and should be covered, but it should not displace eigenvalue and rank practice.

How do I solve eigenvalue questions quickly in GATE?

Use the identities instead of the characteristic polynomial. ∑iλi=tr⁡(A)\sum_i \lambda_i = \operatorname{tr}(A)∑i​λi​=tr(A) and ∏iλi=det⁡A\prod_i \lambda_i = \det A∏i​λi​=detA; AkA^kAk has eigenvalues λk\lambda^kλk, A−1A^{-1}A−1 has 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ, and A+cIA + cIA+cI shifts every eigenvalue to λ+c\lambda + cλ+c. Triangular matrices show their eigenvalues on the diagonal.

What is the rank condition for a system of linear equations?

For Ax=bAx = bAx=b with nnn unknowns, the system has a unique solution when rank⁡(A)=rank⁡([A∣b])=n\operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A \mid b]) = nrank(A)=rank([A∣b])=n. It has infinitely many solutions when rank⁡(A)=rank⁡([A∣b])<n\operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A \mid b]) < nrank(A)=rank([A∣b])<n, and no solution when rank⁡(A)<rank⁡([A∣b])\operatorname{rank}(A) < \operatorname{rank}([A \mid b])rank(A)<rank([A∣b]).

Do I need to learn calculus as well for GATE CSE mathematics?

Yes, but it carries less. Limits score 74.5 and maxima and minima 71.0, while the mean value theorem, continuity and differentiability fall to 63.5. Calculus is the weakest part of the mathematics block in the model, so it deserves one standard form per topic rather than deep practice. Linear algebra and probability deserve the depth.
