Best Books for GATE EE 2027, Subject by Subject

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 23 Oct 20268 min read

If you want one reference text per section for GATE EE 2027, use the standard electrical textbooks: Kreyszig for mathematics, Hayt and Kemmerly for circuits, Hayt and Buck for fields, Oppenheim and Willsky for signals, Kothari and Nagrath for machines and power systems, Nagrath and Gopal for control, Sawhney for measurements, Sedra and Smith for electronics, and Mohan, Undeland and Robbins for power electronics. Read them for method, then move to past papers. This guide gives the list, shows which chapters carry the marks, and tells you where textbooks stop.

Key takeaways

  • One primary textbook per section is enough. A second book is for a doubt, not for a second full read.
  • The 2027 EE syllabus did not change a single examinable word, so a reading list mapped to 2026 still works.
  • Engineering Mathematics is the largest section, 14.8 marks a paper on the mean; give its textbook real time.
  • Measurements averaged 2.6 marks and Electromagnetic Fields 4.4: read those books briefly and selectively.
  • Electric circuits is undercounted by its own mean, because circuit methods sit inside questions counted elsewhere.
  • Textbooks teach the method; past papers teach how GATE asks it. You need both, in that order.

Three terms before the list

A primary text is the one book you read for a section, chapter by chapter, with a pencil. A reference text is a second book you open only when the first explanation does not click. The past-paper layer is the set of GATE questions on a topic, which shows how the idea is examined.

The list below names standard textbooks used across Indian engineering courses. It gives author and title only. Editions and prices are left out because they change. Several of these books have gained co-authors in recent editions; any recent edition covers the GATE syllabus lines.

Before you buy anything, download the official EE syllabus from gate2027.iitm.ac.in. GATE Guide is an independent site with no connection to IIT Madras or the GATE office. The 2026 and 2027 EE documents were compared word by word: no phrase added, none removed, none reworded. The only change is that "Butterwoth" is now spelt "Butterworth".

A worked example

Suppose you have 100 hours for the first textbook pass over the technical sections. Share them in proportion to the mean marks, out of the 85 technical marks:

hours for a section=100×its mean marks85\text{hours for a section} = 100 \times \frac{\text{its mean marks}}{85}hours for a section=100×85its mean marks​

Engineering Mathematics gets 100×14.8/85≈17.4100 \times 14.8 / 85 \approx 17.4100×14.8/85≈17.4 hours. Measurements gets about 3.1, and Electromagnetic Fields about 5.2.

Trap: Electric Circuits gets only about 10.1 hours by this formula, and that is too few. Its own count is 43 marks over five papers, but nine more questions worth 16 marks were counted in other sections with circuits as the runner-up. Diode networks, wattmeter readings and a thyristor in an LC circuit all need circuit methods first.

The GATE EE subject-wise weightage guide explains each mean and its range in full.

How to read a textbook for GATE

  1. Map before you read. Print the two-page syllabus and tick only the chapters that match a line.
  2. Read for procedures. Write each method as numbered steps: a Thevenin equivalent, a per-phase induction motor calculation, a Routh table, a buck converter's duty ratio.
  3. Fix conventions early. Decide once how you write phasors (RMS or peak) and per-unit bases. Mixing two books mid-topic is how a correct method gives a wrong number.
  4. Switch to past papers after the first pass. Don't finish a whole textbook before you touch a GATE question.
  5. Revise from your notes, not the book. Your one-page summaries and the GATE EE formula sheet are faster to revise than any chapter.

Remember: the syllabus is the chapter list, and for 2027 it is the same list as 2026. A chapter earns your time only if it maps to a line in that document.

Mathematics, circuits, fields and signals

Kreyszig covers every Engineering Mathematics line. Calculus, linear algebra and probability drew marks in all five papers. Stokes's theorem, Laurent series, variation of parameters and the Poisson and binomial distributions are named but appear in no counted question; read them anyway, since a zero is not permission to skip. The engineering mathematics formula sheet is a good companion.

For circuits, Hayt and Kemmerly is the primary text. Transient response of DC and AC networks was the one circuits group examined in all five papers. Every counted network-theorem question was a Thevenin or Norton equivalent.

Hayt and Buck covers fields. Keep it short: magnetic circuits, reluctance and inductance are named but drew no counted question. Oppenheim and Willsky covers signals; the Z transform has drawn more marks than the Laplace transform here.

Machines, power systems and control

Kothari and Nagrath serves two sections. In machines, three-phase induction machines held 17 of the section's 42 marks and were the only group set in all five papers. In power systems, economic load dispatch and symmetrical components with fault analysis were set in all five. The heaviest power-systems group held only 9 of 53 marks, so cover that book broadly rather than deeply.

Nagrath and Gopal covers control. Bode plots and the state space model share the lead. Control is set as MCQ more than any other technical section, 20 of its 27 counted questions, so most of its marks carry negative marking. Read for exactness.

In one line: read the machines and power-systems books broadly, and the control book exactly.

Measurements, electronics and power electronics

Sawhney is the measurements text; read bridges, power and energy measurement, instrument transformers and error analysis. Sedra and Smith covers diodes, amplifiers and op-amps; op-amps were the one electronics group set in all five papers, with 14 of the section's 43 marks. Mano covers the combinational and sequential logic. Oscillators, active filters, VCOs and timers, and Schmitt triggers with sample-and-hold drew no counted mark, so past papers cannot prepare you for them; the textbook must.

Mohan, Undeland and Robbins covers converters and inverters, with Rashid for doubts. Read the discontinuous-conduction case carefully, because that is where the simple gain formula stops working.

Where textbooks stop, and where the GATE EE 2027 book fits

A textbook explains the equal area criterion once. It will not show you how GATE framed it, or which neighbouring idea it was paired with. That layer comes from past papers, studied topic by topic. The GATE EE important topics guide shows the method.

Quick revision

  1. One primary textbook per section; a second only for doubts.
  2. The 2027 EE syllabus is word for word the 2026 one; your 2026 chapter map still holds.
  3. Give Engineering Mathematics the most hours: 14.8 marks a paper on the mean.
  4. Circuits is undercounted; read it before machines, electronics and power electronics.
  5. Keep Measurements and Electromagnetic Fields short and selective.
  6. Read control exactly: most of its questions are MCQs with negative marking.
  7. Switch to topic-wise past papers right after each first pass.
  8. Revise from your own notes and a formula sheet, not from the textbook.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best book for GATE Electrical Engineering preparation?

No single book covers the whole EE paper well. Use one standard textbook per section, such as Kothari and Nagrath for machines and power systems, Nagrath and Gopal for control, Sedra and Smith for analog electronics and Mohan, Undeland and Robbins for power electronics. Read only the chapters the official syllabus names, then move to past papers topic by topic.

Has the GATE EE syllabus changed for 2027, and do I need new books?

No examinable word changed. The 2026 and 2027 EE syllabus documents were compared word by word and no phrase was added, removed or reworded; the only change is a corrected spelling of Butterworth. Any standard textbook you mapped to the 2026 syllabus still maps to 2027, so there is no reason to buy new books for the change.

Which book is best for electrical machines in GATE EE?

Kothari and Nagrath, Electric Machines, or P. S. Bimbhra, Electrical Machinery, as the primary text, with Fitzgerald and Kingsley's Electric Machinery for doubts. Give three-phase induction machines the most time: it was the only machines topic group examined in all five counted papers from 2022 to 2026, with 17 of the section's 42 marks.

Are textbooks enough for GATE EE or do I need previous year papers?

You need both, in that order. A textbook builds the method, such as a Thevenin equivalent, a symmetrical-component fault calculation or a Bode plot. Past papers show which forms GATE repeats. In the five counted EE papers, 105 of the 275 technical questions were numerical-answer type, so method and speed matter more than recognising options.

Which book should I read for Electrical and Electronic Measurements?

A. K. Sawhney, A Course in Electrical and Electronic Measurements and Instrumentation, is the standard Indian text. Keep the reading short: the section averaged 2.6 marks a paper across the five counted papers, the smallest in the EE paper. Focus on bridges, power and energy measurement, instrument transformers and error analysis.
