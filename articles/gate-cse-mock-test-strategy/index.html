GATE CSE Mock Tests: How to Take, Score and Analyse Them

By MD ANISH AHAMADUpdated 17 Sep 20268 min read

A GATE CSE mock test is only as useful as the analysis that follows it. Sit every mock in one 180-minute block at your exam's clock time. Score it with the official negative marking before you read a single solution. Then log every wrong, skipped and slow question by subtopic. After three mocks, that log becomes your real revision list.

Key takeaways

  • A useful mock copies the official pattern: 65 questions, 100 marks, and a realistic mix of MCQ, MSQ and NAT.
  • Start full mocks once every subject has been covered once. Take the first two as diagnostics, then one or two a week.
  • Attempt General Aptitude first, make two passes through the technical section, and keep the last 10–15 minutes for NAT and MSQ answers.
  • Score honestly, with MCQ penalties, no partial credit for MSQs and NAT answers at the stated precision.
  • The day after each mock, write down the cause of every mistake and re-study those subtopics before the next one.

What a good mock paper looks like

The real CS paper is 3 hours, 65 questions and 100 marks. General Aptitude has 10 questions for 15 marks, Engineering Mathematics is about 13 marks and the core subjects about 72. Questions are MCQ (one correct of four), MSQ (one or more correct, no partial credit) or NAT (a typed number with no options). Only MCQs carry negative marks: −1/3 for a 1-mark question and −2/3 for a 2-mark question. Confirm the pattern at gate2027.iitm.ac.in.

A mock that is too easy, or too heavy in one question type, trains the wrong pacing. By the book's analysis, the 2025–2026 papers ran at about 34 MCQ, 12 MSQ and 19 NAT. Recent subject shares, averaged over 2018–2026, look roughly like this:

Block Questions (1-mark + 2-mark) Marks
General Aptitude 5 + 5 15
Engineering Mathematics 3 + 5 13
Programming and Data Structures 4 + 3 10
Algorithms, Operating System, COA 3 + 3 each 9 each
Computer Networks, TOC, Databases 2 + 3 each 8 each
Compiler Design 2 + 2 6
Digital Logic 1 + 2 5

These are analytical averages, not official figures. Real papers vary by about two marks per subject either way.

When to start and how many to take

In a 20-week plan from mid-September, full mocks start once every subject has been covered once:

Period Mocks Purpose
End of November 1 Diagnostic: build a subject scorecard
Mid-December 1 Second diagnostic after re-studying weak subtopics
Mid-December to late January 8, one or two a week Mocks with a different focus each, each followed by an analysis day
Final two weeks None One timed re-attempt of your weakest mock's hardest questions

The first two mocks are not about the score. Their job is to show which subjects and subtopics are weak. The GATE CSE preparation strategy article shows where these mocks fit around concept study and PYQ practice.

How to sit a mock

Sit it as if it were the exam: one 180-minute sitting with no breaks, at the same clock time as your slot, with the answer key out of reach. Then follow a fixed attempt order.

  1. General Aptitude, about 15 minutes. Quick recognition items first. Mark any 2-mark GA item that has no plan after 90 seconds.
  2. Technical first pass, about 100 minutes. Go in order. Budget roughly 1.5 minutes for a 1-mark question and 3 minutes for a 2-mark question. Anything without a plan after 60–90 seconds gets marked and skipped. Defer large hand executions.
  3. Second pass, about 50 minutes. Take 2-mark NAT and MSQ questions first (no penalty), then 2-mark MCQs where an option can be eliminated, then 1-mark leftovers.
  4. Last 10–15 minutes. Enter a best answer for every NAT and MSQ. Leave an MCQ blank unless you can rule out at least one option.

The budget adds up. GA takes 15 minutes, 25 one-mark technical questions take about 37.5 and 30 two-mark questions take about 90, which leaves roughly 35 minutes of buffer. More than 5 minutes on one question in the first pass is the most common way to lose that buffer. The MCQ, MSQ and NAT strategy article explains the guessing arithmetic behind step 4.

How to score honestly

Score with the answer key before reading any solution, and apply the official rules exactly.

  • MCQ: −1/3 for a wrong 1-mark answer and −2/3 for a wrong 2-mark answer.
  • MSQ: full marks only for exactly the correct set of options. One extra or one missing option scores zero.
  • NAT: correct only at the stated precision. A right method with a unit slip still scores zero.

Here is how quickly penalties add up. Say you got 12 MCQs wrong, six 1-mark and six 2-mark. That costs 6 × 1/3 + 6 × 2/3 = 6 marks, before counting the marks those questions could have earned.

What your score means

A mock score is only as meaningful as the mock's difficulty. For orientation, here is what raw scores have roughly meant in real GATE CS papers in recent years:

Raw score Approximate meaning in recent years What it has typically required
27–35 Qualified; ranks in the several thousands to tens of thousands GA plus the core concepts of five or six subjects, attempted accurately
40–50 Roughly top 3,000–6,000 GA at 12 or more, core concepts of every subject, at most two wrong MCQs
50–60 Roughly top 1,000–3,000 Core and second-tier concepts, mocks consistently at 55 or more, NAT accuracy above 80 %
60–70 Roughly top 300–1,000 Core, second-tier and lower-priority concepts, a disciplined two-pass attempt, MSQ over-selection under control
70–80 Roughly top 50–300 Near-complete syllabus and no repeated mistakes

The general-category qualifying mark for CS has most often been 27–30, and about 30 in 2026 by published cutoffs. A harder paper, such as 2022, 2024 or 2026, pushes every band down by several marks. An easier one, such as 2019 or 2025, pushes it up. Treat these ranges as a rough guide only.

How to analyse a mock

Analysis is where the marks come from. Do it the day after the mock, in writing.

  1. List every wrong, skipped and slow question. Slow questions count, because they cost the time that other questions needed.
  2. Log each one by subtopic, not subject. Record its type (MCQ, MSQ or NAT), its marks and the cause. The usual causes are a concept gap, a trap, a convention or unit slip, a misread, or time.
  3. Re-study the three weakest subtopics before the next mock, and drill their question forms under time.
  4. Keep a subject scorecard across mocks, so you can see whether a weakness is closing.

After three mocks, the log is a better revision list than any book's topic list, because it holds your own mistakes. The common mistakes in GATE CSE article lists the traps that show up most often in these logs.

Also look at accuracy against attempts. Turning three wrong 2-mark MCQs into skips gains two marks, and turning them into correct answers gains eight. If your mock scores sit around 45–55, cutting wrong MCQs and NAT slips is usually faster than adding topics.

Give each mock a different job

Ten similar papers teach less than ten papers with different purposes. A useful sequence runs:

  1. High-frequency concepts only.
  2. High-frequency plus second-tier concepts.
  3. Question forms from 2021–2026.
  4. Statement-heavy MCQs and MSQs.
  5. A NAT-heavy paper.
  6. A balanced full-syllabus paper.
  7. A trap-heavy paper.
  8. Overlooked syllabus items.
  9. An exam-like mixed paper.
  10. A final paper on the highest-confidence concepts.

Frequently asked questions

How many mock tests should I take for GATE CSE?

Ten full-length mocks is a solid number if you start in late November. Take the first two as diagnostics a fortnight apart, then one or two a week from mid-December to late January. Each mock needs a full analysis day after it, so taking more mocks without analysing them adds little.

When should I start taking GATE CSE mock tests?

Start once you have covered every subject once, which in a 20-week plan beginning in September is around the end of November. An early full mock is a diagnostic. Its job is to show which subjects and subtopics are weak, not to predict your final score, so do not be discouraged by a low first result.

What is a good score in a GATE CSE mock test?

It depends on how hard the mock is. As an approximate guide from recent real papers, the general qualifying mark has usually been about 27 to 30, a raw score of 50 to 60 has meant roughly the top 1,000 to 3,000, and 60 to 70 roughly the top 300 to 1,000. Harder papers push these bands down.

How do I analyse a GATE mock test?

Score it first with the official marking scheme, then read the solutions. The next day, write down every wrong, skipped and slow question with its subtopic, question type and cause, such as a concept gap, a trap, a unit slip or overspent time. Re-study the weakest subtopics before the next mock.

Should I take a full mock test in the last week before GATE?

It is better not to. Finish your full mocks about two weeks before your paper. In the final fortnight, revise from a last-minute sheet and your error log, and do one timed re-attempt of the hardest questions from your weakest mock in the same time slot as your exam.
