Syllabus alert

Computer Networks lost a third of its topic list for 2027

If you prepare from a 2026 book, you will spend days on protocols that are no longer in the syllabus, and under-prepare the ones that now absorb the entire Computer Networks allocation.

No longer in the 2027 syllabus
  • OSI / TCP-IP stack enumeration
  • ARP, DHCP, ICMP
  • UDP
  • SMTP, FTP, Email
  • Framing, Ethernet bridging
  • Shortest-path and flooding routing
Now carrying the full weight
  • TCP flow and congestion control
  • IPv4, fragmentation, CIDR, NAT
  • Distance-vector and link-state routing
  • Error detection, MAC, Ethernet
  • Switching and performance metrics
  • Socket API, DNS, HTTP

Digital Logic and Computer Organisation also changed. The tabular minimisation method and hardwired versus microprogrammed control unit design are now named explicitly, and those have almost no previous-year history to practise from. The book writes fresh questions for exactly those gaps.

Questions people ask
Are these real previous-year questions?

No, and that is deliberate. Every question is newly written. Past papers are freely available and you should solve them too. What this book adds is the analysis of which concepts those papers keep returning to, and fresh questions built on exactly those patterns, so you are practising prediction rather than memory.

Is the 2027 syllabus really different?

Yes, in three sections. Computer Networks lost a large part of its topic list, Computer Organisation gained control-unit design and cache mapping as explicitly named items, and Digital Logic named the tabular minimisation method for the first time. The book shows the old and new wording side by side and re-weights everything accordingly.

Can you guarantee these questions will appear?

No. Nobody honestly can. The book gives you a ranked, reasoned view of what the evidence supports, with confidence figures that are explicitly analytical estimates rather than probabilities. Exact-question confidence is capped at 35% throughout for that reason.

I have only two months left. Is it still useful?

Yes. There is a short-path route built in: the Top 100 concept list, the do-not-skip checklist, the Tier 1 and Tier 2 questions only, and four of the ten mocks. That plan is written out in the revision section.
